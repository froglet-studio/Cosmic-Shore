# What motion alone says - the literature behind the emotion probe's features

Every feature in `common/affect.py` is tied to a finding below. "Supports" says what the finding actually
licenses; several are about ANIMACY or AROUSAL rather than a named emotion, and the probe's emotion words
are our own mapping on top.

| feature(s) | source | what the source found | what it supports here |
|---|---|---|---|
| speed / direction change, `burst`, `unpredict`, `curvature` | Tremoulet & Feldman 2000, *Perception* 29:943 ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12184353_Perception_of_Animacy_from_the_Motion_of_a_Single_Object)) | a single dot reads as ALIVE from a change in speed and a change in direction, more so when its orientation re-aligns to the new path | self-propelled change = animacy; a creature that never changes speed reads as an object |
| social roles from motion, `size_log`, `approach`, `pursuit` | Heider & Simmel 1944 ([pdf](https://www.coli.uni-saarland.de/courses/agentinteraction/contents/papers/Heider44.pdf)) | 3 shapes moving around a box: viewers agree the BIG triangle is a bully and the small shapes are timid/friendly - from motion and relative size only | size + who-moves-toward-whom carries valence and dominance |
| `pursuit`, `gaze` | Gao, Newman & Scholl 2009, *Cogn Psych* ([pdf](https://perception.yale.edu/papers/09-Gao-Newman-Scholl-CogPsych.pdf)) | chasing is seen when the wolf heads at the sheep; detection collapses as "chasing subtlety" (max deviation from heat-seeking) grows, chance by 120 deg | heat-seeking motion = being chased; our `pursuit` counts velocity within ~32 deg of the pilot |
| `gaze` (published facing) | Gao, McCarthy & Scholl 2010, the wolfpack effect ([pdf](http://www.stat.ucla.edu/~taogao/static/pdf/The%20wolfpack%20effect_Gao%20McCarthy%20Scholl_2010.pdf)) | randomly moving darts that all POINT at a moving disc look like a pack pursuing it, and change how people move | facing you is read as attending to you, even with no approach |
| `loom`, `tau_inv`, `view_fill`, `converge` | Schiff 1962; Lee's tau; mouse looming (e.g. [PMC3809337](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3809337/)); infants + a looming network ([PMC11126809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11126809/)) | an expanding dark disc triggers innate flight/freeze in < 250 ms; faster approach -> stronger defence; tau = angle / expansion rate gives time-to-contact. BUT across naturalistic video, looming predicted subjective AROUSAL, not fear or valence specifically | looming is the arousal axis of threat; it needs a negative-valence cue (attention, pursuit) to become terror rather than excitement - which is exactly how our probe ended up using it |
| `size_log`, `roundness`, `bounce` | Lorenz's Kindchenschema; Glocker et al. 2009 ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221768895_Baby_Schema_in_Infant_Faces_Induces_Cuteness_Perception_and_Motivation_for_Caretaking_in_Adults)); robot-motion work ([Saerbeck & Bartneck 2010](https://www.bartneck.de/publications/2010/perceptionAffectElicitedRobotMotion/saerbeckBartneckHRI2010.pdf)) | round face, big head-to-body, small = cute and caretaking; "light, bouncy" robot motion reads as happy | small + round + bouncy = cute. NOTE: the baby schema is about FACES and proportions; a big head relative to the body is not visible to a point-motion probe at all |
| `accel_rel`, `jerk_rel`, `speed_rel` | Pollick et al. 2001, *Cognition* 82:B51 ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222660068_Perceiving_affect_from_arm_movement)); Saerbeck & Bartneck 2010 | arm movements: higher arousal = shorter, greater velocity/acceleration/jerk; robot motion: acceleration correlates with perceived arousal | the energy axis (calm vs excited) |
| `accel_rel`, `jerk_rel` (Time), `unpredict` (Space), `size_log`/mass (Weight), `regularity`/`synchrony` (Flow) | Laban Effort: Weight (strong/light), Time (sudden/sustained), Space (direct/indirect), Flow (bound/free) ([LMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban_movement_analysis)) | the four effort factors carry the "intention and dynamic quality" of movement and are used for emotion recognition | majestic = strong + sustained + direct + free; playful = light + sudden + indirect + free; eerie = bound |
| `synchrony`, `regularity`, `mimicry` | the uncanny valley (Mori 1970; [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncanny_valley)) - robotic motion "too smooth, too regular, too precise" | almost-but-not-quite-living motion is eerie; motion amplifies it | a living group has individual noise; identical gait across members, or a thing copying your moves, is the uncanny cue |
| `stillness`, `burst`, `sneak` | predator stalking and prey freezing ([Freeze for action, RSTB](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/372/1718/20160206/23227/Freeze-for-action-neurobiological-mechanisms-in-animal-and-human-freezing); [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1915504116)); Caro 1994 on cheetah stalks | stalkers approach slowly and freeze when the prey looks; sudden motion after stillness signals the strike; fast expansion, not size, triggers escape | still-then-sudden, and moving only when unwatched, are the stalking cues |
| `tracking`, `mimicry` | Johnson, Slaughter & Carey 1998 (contingent interaction makes infants treat a blob as an agent) | contingent responsiveness to the viewer is an agency cue | a thing that keeps its distance to you is attending; a thing that passes by is indifferent - the line between awe and threat |
| graded score: valence, arousal | Russell 1980, circumplex of affect | emotions lie on a valence x arousal plane | errors between neighbouring emotions are graded, not wrong |
| graded score: threat | Fanselow & Lester 1988, predatory imminence continuum | defence changes with imminence: pre-encounter -> post-encounter (predator detected) -> circa-strike | menacing = post-encounter (it is there and attending); terrifying = circa-strike (contact imminent) |
| approach/avoid | approach-avoidance motivation (Elliot 2006) | | the sign of `approach` |

## What the literature does NOT give us

- No study maps these features to the SIX words we use (cute / playful / eerie / majestic / menacing /
  terrifying). Most affect-from-motion work uses basic emotions (happy, sad, angry, afraid) of the MOVER,
  not the emotion EVOKED in a watcher. We need the evoked emotion. The mapping is ours.
- Kindchenschema is about faces and body proportions. A point probe sees size and an authored aspect ratio;
  it cannot see eyes, a forehead or a head-to-body ratio.
- Looming's best-supported effect is AROUSAL. Fear needs a valence cue on top.
