# field — designed fields + boids (no learning)

Code: `Tools/NCA/field_swarm.py` (model + publish), `field_probes.py` (extra probes, ablations),
`field_showcase.py` (self-contained playback page `showcase.html`). Everything here is regenerated by
`python Tools/NCA/field_swarm.py publish && python Tools/NCA/field_probes.py && python Tools/NCA/field_showcase.py`
in about 4 minutes on 4 CPU cores. There is nothing to train: `params.json` is the whole model.

## What it is

Every tadpole is a boid — separation (2.0), alignment (5.0), arrive-to-home with velocity feed-forward,
per-element top speed (the World's 0.8 / 0.8 / 0.8 / 2.0) — steering to a HOME in a body-plan attractor:

1. **Slots.** The plan's own units, animated through its 8 frames (ping-pong when the loop does not close,
   6 steps per frame), are matched to tadpoles by Hungarian assignment on the scorer's own cost (Huber
   position + 60·element + 25·domain-region), with a stickiness bonus. Re-solved every 8 steps or when the
   membership changes. A tadpole's prism / tier / facing / spindle blend toward its slot's (decode() maps the
   prism into the tadpole's own element identity, so a slot can never break element).
2. **Field.** Per plan, per element, an RBF density over that element's target units. A tadpole with no slot
   climbs its element's gradient; `mode=field` uses only this (ablation below).
3. **Plan choice = element ratios + time hysteresis.** The living majority names the plan; a new majority must
   hold 12 steps before the swarm commits. While contested, it neither lays nor molts — that is exactly what
   made the learned rules flip back.
4. **Morph.** A committed switch re-assigns everyone to the new plan; they travel there inside a vortex about
   the swarm's vertical axis that peaks mid-morph while the old plan's field fades out (60 steps).
5. **Composition, mass-conserving.** Laying breeds TRUE (element and domain = parent's): a parent of a deficit
   (element, domain) lays beside the nearest empty slot of that kind, up to the plan's unit count, so a body
   grows from its holes. **Relaxed constraint: molting** — a tadpole of a SURPLUS element changes element into a
   deficit one over 10 steps (its crystal re-forms; domain never changes). Without it a switched swarm keeps the
   old plan's leftovers forever, because nothing may die on a clock. Nothing dies on its own; deaths only come
   from outside (the eval cull, a strike).
6. **Vessel reaction.** `model.predators = [(centre, radius, velocity)]`. Tadpoles near the ship *or near its
   path within 10 steps ahead* are startled; startle relays neighbour-to-neighbour (a school's startle wave) and
   decays. Startled tadpoles flee sideways off the ship's line with a swirl around it, per element: Time darts
   (2.0), Space jets (1.4), Charge holds (0.6), Mass shoulders through (0.5); startled Charge plates flash to
   DANGER, and the **pufferfish plan inflates** (homes ×1 + 0.45·threat) and deflates when the ship is gone.

Geometry-only diagonals: 0.07 / 0.09 / 0.43 / 1.41 against 12–26 off-diagonal. Every swarm has exactly its
plan's headcount and element mix (e.g. whale el=[19,126,25,22] vs plan [19,126,25,22]). Zero deaths except
the cull.

**Why the 8th fails, and why I think the yardstick is wrong there, not the swarm.** The dragonfly plan is
[Charge 2, Mass 3, Space 18, Time 53]. The yardstick's cull (`lose_majority(..., to=Mass)`, mode "excess")
removes Time until it is one behind Mass, leaving **[2, 3, 19, 2]: Space is 73% of the survivors.** By the
brief's own rule (the majority element picks the plan) the right body is the jellyfish, which the swarm grows
(19.9 vs 63–75 elsewhere). For any swarm grown at the dragonfly's true mix, "closest to the whale" here can
only pass by breaking the majority rule. The earlier learned rules that passed this test (E8) had grown a
dragonfly with the wrong mix. The `mode=field, molt=0` ablation below passes 8/8 for the same bad reason (its
dragonfly never grew its Space wing-runners: 56/76 with Space 3, and its own geometry is closer to the
pufferfish) — **that 8/8 is spurious and I do not claim it.**

With a cull that really hands Mass the majority (every other element culled below Mass: [2,3,2,2] left),
**dragonfly → whale passes**: 9 survivors regrow a 178-tadpole whale scored 12.2 against ≥ 60 elsewhere.
Strict-cull switches: **4/4**.

**Switch latency** (steps after the cull until the new plan is closest, scored every 10): 30 / 20 / 20 (/0 for
the strict whale). The commit itself waits the 12-step dwell; the morph spectacle runs 60 steps.

## Probes

Strike (`swarm_probe.probe`, a third of the body removed): heal **1.07 / 1.04 / 0.92 / 1.06**
(whale / jelly / puffer / dragonfly; 1 = back to the pre-strike score; >1 = better than before). Headcount back
to the plan's in every case (192 / 88 / 179 / 76) within the 120-step window.

Predator pass (new; `field_swarm.predator_pass`): a sphere of 0.6× the swarm's RMS radius flies straight through
the centroid at 3 voxels/step (≈2–4× the tadpoles' top speed), killing nothing. "touched" = share of the school
that was ever inside it; compared with the same swarm with reactions switched OFF:

| plan | touched, reacting | touched, inert | worst score during pass (react / inert) | re-formed after (steps) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| whale | 0.28 | 0.37 | 5.0 / 3.5 | 4 |
| jellyfish | **0.08** | 0.31 | 4.9 / 3.6 | 8 |
| pufferfish | **0.11** | 0.28 | 10.6 / 4.0 (it inflates) | 18 |
| dragonfly | **0.11** | 0.36 | 8.1 / 6.2 | 9 |

The whale barely parts by design (heavy). Reacting costs shape during the pass and is back within ~4–18 steps.

* Slots buy crispness (3–8× lower divergence) but the field alone already tells the plans apart.
* Molting is what makes a switched body clean: without it whale→jelly ends at 33 (the old mass plates stay).
* Dwell barely matters on the yardstick (the cull is one-shot); it matters when a predator eats tadpoles one at
  a time and the majority flickers.

## Cost and port

numpy, one swarm, per step: **0.9 ms (76) / 1.2 ms (88) / 3.7 ms (179) / 4.3 ms (192)**, dominated by the O(n²)
neighbour pass and the Hungarian solve (every 8 steps). In Unity/Burst: boids + startle relay over the existing
fauna spatial index / Burst density grid (O(n·k)); the slot table is the target JSON baked per plan per frame;
the assignment runs every ~8 frames per swarm — Hungarian on ≤192 is fine in C# (sub-ms), or swap for an auction /
greedy-nearest per (element, domain) group; the field mode needs no assignment at all. Per-tadpole state is 8
floats (velocity, slot, molt, startle, age). No GPU needed.

## What a player would see

A loose knot of 16 tadpoles that, within ~60 steps, lays its way out into a recognisable creature that swims in
place on its 8-frame cycle (the jellyfish's bell pulses, the dragonfly's Time runners lap the wings). Fly at it:
the school opens a tunnel ahead of the ship before it arrives and closes behind it; a white startle wave ripples
out from the contact; the pufferfish swells and turns its plates red. Ram it and the hole fills from the inside
as new tadpoles bud beside the holes. Eat its majority element and, after a beat of hesitation, the whole body
dissolves into a vortex and reassembles as another animal, with tadpoles visibly molting (re-colouring) along the
way. `showcase.html` plays one scripted life per plan (drag to orbit).

## What failed / caveats

* The 8th yardstick test (explained above). Field-only mode is blurrier and its laying can stall when a needed
  (element, domain) pair has no parent (the dragonfly's Space runners).
* Molting relaxes "element fixed at birth". It is the cleanest mass-conserving answer to surplus; the
  alternative (a surplus tadpole sheds as a crystal) would be an imposed death.
* Body orientation is fixed to the plan's axes because the scorer is not rotation-invariant; in game the
  anchor frame would rotate with the swim heading (free: slots are in the anchor frame).
* Grow-in is only drawn in the showcase (radius ramps with age); the eggs here are born hatched.

## Recommendation for the next round

Ship this as the **behavioural shell** and put learning only where design is weak: (1) keep slots + hysteresis +
molting as the composition controller — it alone solves the switching problem the learned rules failed on;
(2) let a learned or procedural layer generate the *plan* (the slot cloud / field) instead of the motion, e.g.
the NCA growing a target cloud that this controller then fills; (3) port to C#/Burst behind the existing fauna
spatial index first in `mode=field` (no assignment) to prove cost, then add the assignment; (4) fix the
yardstick's dragonfly→whale cull (cull every element below the switch target, as `field_probes.strict_cull`
does) so the 8 tests measure the rule the brief states.
