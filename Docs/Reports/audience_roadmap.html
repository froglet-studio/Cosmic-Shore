One niche at a time, joined into one household

Cosmic Shore already contains a racer, a party game, a hex puzzle, a sniper duel, a kite toy, a painter and a sport, and two of its hulls seat two different kinds of player in one ship. The plan: win the niche we can reach today, then use each niche to bring in the next one through the people its players already play with.

Where it ends: one squad, four kinds of player

Working backwards starts here. A voyage squad is three vessels; a crew vessel puts two people in one of them. These four people already exist in most families and friend groups. Today each of them plays a different game, alone.

Old phone · portrait · one thumbThe puzzlerPlays the Ratel's Dynamo, a hex puzzle built from the Slipstream cards. Every pattern charges the Honeyguide's slips.

PC · mouse or padThe competitorSnipes in Serpent Gulag, duels in Dog Fight; on a voyage, the escort when another squad comes for the Ark.

The connection is mechanical, not social glue added on top: the Ratel's patterns become the Honeyguide's slip charges, and the Honeyguide's hives become the Ratel's raids (Docs/Studios/ONE_THUMB_SEAT_ARCS.md §4). One person's game is the other person's fuel.

The order, and why

The first pin has to be reachable with what is in motion: the Steam Playtest, the hulls that ship, and the one element whose AI is live (Time), so a niche with few players still finds full races. Every later pin is chosen because the players of the pin before it already know someone in it.

What the end state needs, and which pin builds it

Read upward from the voyage. Every requirement is delivered by a pin that also earns its own players, so nothing is built only for the distant goal.

The number that says it is working

Weekly players who played with someone whose main vessel is a different genre

Every pin is judged on its own niche first (wishlists, retention, full lobbies). This one number says whether the niches are joining: it rises only when a racer brings a puzzler, or a puzzler brings a kid. Vessel telemetry already records who flew what; the "main genre" of a player is the element petal of the cards they play most.

Decisions this needs

  1. Steam first or mobile first. ABOUT_PROJECT.md says "mobile-first with PC/console expansion"; everything in motion is Steam-first. This plan takes Steam for pins 1–2 because that is where the launch work, the controllers and the Time AI are, and brings mobile in at pin 3 through the Ratel. The docs should say one thing.
  2. How a phone player pays. Commerce is de-scoped everywhere (R4). Puzzle players expect free-to-play; Steam players expect $15. Pin 3 cannot start until someone decides whether mobile is free with purchases, free with the PC owner's invite, or paid.
  3. A Dynamo card. A puzzle player should be able to start in the Ratel seat from the Arcade, with the AI flying the Honeyguide, not by finding the Vessel Changer in the Toybox. This seats a player; it changes nothing about the vessel, so it keeps the locked rule that a mode never changes the vessel.
  4. Cross-play. Whether a phone and a PC can share a party has not been checked. Pin 3 depends on it.
  5. Steam Remote Play Together. It streams a local seat to a friend who does not own the game. If two humans in one hull works locally (built, untested), a PC owner could hand the Ratel or Wrasse seat to a friend before online crew seats exist. Worth one test.
  6. Children's privacy. Pin 4 invites kids. Age ratings, parental settings and what is collected from a child need settling before it is marketed to families (the privacy report already lists eleven places where policy and code disagree, Docs/Legal/).

Owners to ask before changing their areas: the Ratel and Wrasse seats are Garrett's; anything on UGS or the backend, and activation, go through Shombith, with Caleb heading the first-time experience; the Steam launch report is written for Shombith and Caleb. No step here is assigned to anyone.
