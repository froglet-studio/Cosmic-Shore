Reading it

  • The Steam wishlist count decides almost everything. Doubling it nearly doubles five-year revenue; nothing else comes close. The Playtest earns nothing by Valve's rule, so its whole job is wishlists.
  • Friend invites only pay once the PC base is large. Mobile installs from invites scale with Steam owners, so in the base case (about 20,000 owners) they add little; in the high case they are the largest single source of phone players. The roadmap's order matters for money, not just reach.
  • Paid mobile acquisition does not pay at these numbers. A casual puzzle install costs $2.50–6.00 to buy; the base case earns $0.40 per install. Phone players have to come from the store, the browser Toybox and invites.
  • The base case does not fund a team. About $45–80k a year net is below the cost of one full-time developer in most of the US. Reaching the high case depends on a breakout launch, not on the later streams.
  • Two thresholds sit inside the high case. FMOD's free tier ends at $200k gross a year, and the project's own decision is to buy the licence if it is crossed (Docs/THIRD_PARTY_DECISIONS.md). Steam's cut drops from 30% to 25% only past $10M gross per title, which no scenario reaches.
  • Below the low case is zero. About 20,000 games launched on Steam in 2025 and the median earned about $249. The low case still assumes a paid launch in 2028 and a mobile release in 2029.

Not included

Sales tax and VAT, refunds, payment fees outside the stores, development and marketing costs, investment or grants, merchandise, and any revenue from the browser Toybox (it is treated as a way in, not a revenue stream). Mobile revenue is counted in the month a player installs. Purchases on PC and mobile are switched off today (R4); both streams assume that decision is made.
