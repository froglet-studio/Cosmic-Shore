Paid Early Access on Steam, then free to play everywhere with one account. Players buy episode tokens, bundles or the infinite pass, never more than $120 in all, and merchandise on the web. Gross is what players spend. Net is what reaches Froglet after store fees (and, for merchandise, after the cost of making it), before tax, refunds and running costs.

Reading it

  • Free to play pays back more slowly and keeps growing. In the base case it passes the paid plan over five years, about $499k against $437k gross, and 2031 is the biggest year ($141k, still rising), where the paid plan peaked in 2030 and fell after.
  • Spend per payer is the biggest lever, then how many accounts the free launch brings. Doubling what a paying player buys moves five years from $499k to $789k; doubling the Steam free-to-play launch moves it to $704k. Launch wishlists matter less than they did, because the paid copies stop at 1.0.
  • The $120 ceiling costs almost nothing in five years. The base case's lifetime spend per paying player is $33. Only pass buyers, and players who bundle and then take the pass, reach the ceiling, and that is the $120 the design already plans for. What it gives up is the long tail of heavy spenders most free-to-play games live on, which this design does not have.
  • The web store is worth having, and is limited by the stores' rules. Moving the share bought on the web from none to half adds about $38k net over five years in the base case. Apple's US rules on linking out are still being settled after the December 2025 appeal ruling, and whether a Steam player may buy outside Steam needs checking against Valve's in-game purchase rules before the Steam version links to the web.
  • Merchandise is small until the audience is large. At 3 buyers per 1,000 monthly players it is under $3k a year in the base case and about $125k a year in the high case.
  • The base case still does not fund a team. About $83–112k a year net from 2029. The high case depends on a free launch that brings hundreds of thousands of accounts.

Decisions the model had to guess

  1. What does the pass cost after a player has spent something? The ceiling only holds if the pass costs $120 minus what was already spent, whenever that is less than $90. The model assumes it does.
  2. Does the $15 Early Access price count toward the $120? The model assumes it does, and that Early Access owners keep the game and get whatever free-to-play players get.
  3. What are "the three platforms"? Read here as Steam, iOS and Android on one account, with the web as a store beside them. A console port is kept as a later stream on the same account.
  4. How many episodes a year? Two exist today (Assets/_SO_Assets/XP/EpisodeList.asset). The calculator above shows how the offers compare at each cadence; spend in the scenarios is set per paying player.
  5. Steam and the web store. If Valve requires Steam's own payment for anything bought in the Steam version, Steam players' web share is zero. The repo already has a web checkout for episodes (Docs/MENU_PROGRESSION_AND_IAP.md §5), switched off with the rest of commerce (R4).

Not included

Sales tax and VAT, refunds, development and marketing costs, investment or grants, and ads. App stores are charged at the 15% small-business rate (under $1M a year); the high case passes that on mobile from 2029, which would lower its net. Token spend is spread over a player's first four years (half, a quarter, 15%, 10%), and spend after 2031 is not counted.
