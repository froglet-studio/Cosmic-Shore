# CRYSTAL WORMHOLE — an attractor and a repulsor, one smoothly graded warp of space

> Space crystals fold the HyperSea into a **wormhole**: an **attractor** and a **repulsor** joined by
> one throat. Fly into the attractor and you come out of the repulsor. Nothing about it is a surface
> — no horizon, no shadow, no ring, no rim, no edge where a field starts or stops: what a pilot sees
> is a smoothly graded warping of space that swells around them as they shrink into it, and the pair
> ends the way an electron and a hole do — they attract, spiral together and interfere into nothing.
> The HyperSea is far too big for black holes to make sense; these are not black holes. Status:
> **built 2026-10-08, not yet run in the editor.**

## 1. The pair

An **attractor** (at the cell's centre) and a **repulsor** (500 u up the axis) — `BlackHole`s from the
gravity-well engine, both configured as **smooth wells** of the same strength and opposite sign. The
attractor's `Throat` is the repulsor and vice versa. A prism the attractor takes into its core (half
the throat radius) is carried to the same point in the repulsor's core with its velocity and driven
back out — nothing is destroyed, the pair conserves the mass it moves. A Butterfly-fold
`WormholeMouth` pair is seated on the poles (children of them, so they move with them), carrying
every pilot free (no toll, no owner — a fold of the HyperSea belongs to nobody).

## 2. No interface anywhere

Every place the black-hole version drew an edge is a gradient here:

| Was (a black hole) | Is (a smooth well) |
|---|---|
| Paczyński–Wiita pull, singular at the horizon | Plummer: `a = −GM·r / (r² + ε²)^1.5`, ε = the throat — zero at the centre, peaks at ε/√2, smooth everywhere |
| Capture at the horizon | a core the attractor carries through; the repulsor drives its core out |
| Lense–Thirring frame dragging (∝ 1/r³) | none — a horizon's, and there is none |
| Tides `k/max(r, r_s)³` | `k/(r² + ε²)^1.5` — finite through the centre |
| Schwarzschild ray trace: shadow, photon ring, Einstein ring | the graded lens (§2.1) |
| A mouth: an opaque sphere with a fresnel rim | the seamless mouth (§2.2) |
| Warp field: hard floor and cap, poles combined by `min` (a crease where one takes over) | soft floor and cap; poles combined as a PRODUCT (§2.3) |
| Felt pull held at the throat's surface value inside it | Plummer-softened by the throat (§3) |

### 2.1 The graded lens

Each view ray is turned toward a well by `α = A·(w / max(D, w))·u·e^(−u²/2)`, `u = b/w` (b the ray's
impact parameter, w the core width, D the eye's distance), signed: an attractor magnifies what lies
behind it by up to `1/(1−A)`, a repulsor shrinks it by `1/(1+A)`. In screen angle the map's slope is
`1 − A·(1−u²)e^(−u²/2)`, which for `A < 1` never reaches zero: **the image never folds** — no caustic,
no ring. The bend is gone by `u = 4` (0.0013), so the 4w lens sphere has no seam. **Every smooth lens
sums the deflections of every smooth well** (the global bank, published once a frame), so where two
lens spheres overlap they draw the same picture, and an attractor and a repulsor at one point cancel
exactly. Shipped `A = 0.6` (2.5× at the attractor's centre, 0.62× at the repulsor's), `w` = the throat.

### 2.2 The seamless mouth

`Wormhole.shader` with `_SoftEdge > 0` (`WormholeSeamless.mat`, 0.65): alpha-blended, no depth write,
in the transparent queue, and its alpha is graded by the view ray's impact parameter — opaque at the
centre of the disc, gone at its silhouette (smoothstep, eased twice) — with no rim and no transit
flare. The view beyond dissolves into the world; nothing says "surface". Because the lens pass copies
the scene AFTER the transparents, the mouth is bent with everything else. The Butterfly fold's
`Wormhole.mat` is unchanged (`_SoftEdge 0`, opaque, its domain-tinted rim).

### 2.3 The field

`RadialWarp` is now `s = √(min² + core²)`, `core = max·y/(1 + y⁴)^¼`, `y = (r/R)^k / max` — proportional to
distance near the centre, saturating toward its maximum, no crease. With poles registered the field
is read around them and composed as `s = Π s_i^{a_i}` (each pole's live amplitude), so it is smooth
everywhere and a pole at amplitude 0 is flat space. The cell ships `R = 350`, floor 0.01.

## 3. The felt pull

Vessels feel `Sign · k · cruise² · R_t · s · r / (r² + R_t²)^1.5` (× amplitude) in their own frame —
inverse-square far out when unwarped, 1/r under the radial warp, zero at the centre, no edge. Measured
in each hull's cruise speed because the fleet's spans 35 → 216 u/s. Ceiling `1.3 × cruise`. Shipped
`k = 4`, reach 12 throats (312 u). Asserted on the shipped numbers for cruise 35/60/180: **the attractor
carries a hull in at ~2.3× its cruise; the repulsor holds off any hull at cruise and a hull boosting
at 2–2.5× gets through** (~4–21 s by hull).

## 4. Amplitude

`BlackHole.Amplitude` (0 = flat space) multiplies everything a pole does: its gravity (and so the felt
pull), its tides, its lens strength and its warp depth (the field reads it per pole). It is the one
dial the life of the pair turns.

## 5. Life: formation and annihilation

One curve of progress `p` (0 standing, 1 gone), `CrystalWormhole.Curve` — pure and tested:

- separation `(1 − p)^0.75` — the poles close, faster at the end;
- spiral `2π · turns · p²` about an axis across the pair (2.5 turns);
- envelope `(1 − p)^1.5`;
- beat `φ = 2π · cycles · p²` (7 cycles, quickening); the attractor's amplitude is `env·(1 + 0.5 sin φ)`,
  the repulsor's `env·(1 − 0.5 sin φ)` — **anti-phase**: one pole's warp deepens while the other's
  relaxes, so the warping and unwarping convolute;
- at `p = 1` they meet as equal and opposite wells: the summed lens and the summed pull cancel,
  every amplitude is 0, and space is flat — **destructive interference into nothing.**

**Annihilation** runs `p: 0 → 1` (9 s); **formation** runs the same curve backwards (6 s) — the pair
grows out of nothing at the midpoint and spirals apart. The mouths scale with the envelope. A pair
turning round mid-animation starts from where it is (no jump). When its world retires (the Cell
Selector), it annihilates inside the suction (0.9 s). `lifetimeSeconds` (0 = until retired) ends it
on its own. Console: `blackhole annihilate [seconds]`.

## 6. What is open

- **Forming it with crystals.** The fiction is that players open a wormhole with space crystals; the
  cell is a stand-in. `CrystalWormhole.Open(host, attractorPos, repulsorPos, settings)` is the whole
  API a crystal mechanic needs — which crystals, how many, where the two ends land and who triggers the
  end are design decisions not taken yet.
- **Untested in the editor.** Verify: Cell Selector → *Crystal Wormhole* → watch the pair form (6 s) →
  fly at the attractor: space swells, the view of the far side fades in at the centre with no edge,
  you speed up and pass → you come out of the repulsor, are pushed away and grow → fly back at the
  repulsor at cruise: it holds you off; boost through → `blackhole annihilate` and watch the poles
  spiral together, beat and vanish. Also: lay trail near the attractor and watch it come out of the
  repulsor.
- **Stated limits.** A pixel whose opaque scene depth is in front of a pole's centre is left unbent
  (the lens's depth test); the Scarab's cruise reads 25 (its speed is not throttle-driven), so it
  feels gentler poles; the scale model is not to scale.
