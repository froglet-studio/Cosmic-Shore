REV 56 · NOTES
Phone in portrait too
ONE UI, THREE LAYOUTS
  • PC and phone are not two UIs: the same screens, kit components and colour tokens. Each canvas switches its LAYOUT.
  • PC, console, iPad: the 1920 x 1080 layout. Phone, landscape: the 800 x 450 layout (Rev 52-55). Phone, portrait: a 450 x 800 layout (these boards).
  • Any screen without a layout for the device draws as today: the PC layout shrunk (PhoneRect, Draw At PC Scale).
HOW THE GAME KNOWS
  • A phone: PhoneScale.IsPhone - the screen's SHORT side in points, from the device's reported density (Screen.dpi), under 600. An iPhone is about 390, an iPad 820, a 1080p monitor about 1800; a phone with no dpi is judged at 2x, a desktop never.
  • Portrait: the screen is taller than it is wide (Screen.width < Screen.height). AdaptiveCanvasScaler would pick the 450 x 800 reference and each PhoneRect its portrait pose.
  • Checked at start and on every resolution change, so turning the phone over re-lays the menu.
WHAT PORTRAIT CHANGES
  • The five tabs move from the left rail to a bottom tab bar (the thumb's reach when held upright); the friends orb and Settings move to the top bar.
  • Columns stack: Configure's picture on top, its controls under it; Hangar's fleet becomes a strip; Arcade's games go two across.
  • The one action that commits (Start game, Play again) is pinned at the bottom, at the thumb.
  • Sheets (the party, controls, abilities) rise from the bottom, as in landscape.
NOT NOW: WHAT IT WOULD TAKE
  • The game locks the screen to landscape (DeviceOrientationHandler sets LandscapeLeft and turns autorotate off); the Ratel's Dynamo board draws a quarter turn inside landscape (HONEYGUIDE_LAB.md, 2026-10-22). Portrait needs the lock lifted for the menus and the games that play upright, and a camera and HUD for each such game - the Honeyguide and Ratel first.
  • PhoneRect gains a portrait pose; each screen's generator writes it; the scaler picks it when the screen is upright.
