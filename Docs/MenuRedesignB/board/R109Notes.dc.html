AskedShombith, 2026-11-13, on the built Rev 99 C: "I like C but the actual device it looks too earth like, it should be something completely different and modern and never seen before." And: take the lighting and design from the pilot scenes set inside the Ark.
KeptEverything but the device: the hangar bench, the camera, the pick-up, the crew page and a pilot's file, the pad. The display stays an upright rectangle when held, about the same size, so ArkPilotsPage and its generator do not change.
Where the cues come fromThe in-Ark pilot scenes: Zodiac's chart table that glows at its rim, Needle's and Galleon's brass fittings, Sundial's glass, the elemental crystals' cut light, and the game's own prisms. No slab, no bezel, no keys: nothing a phone or tablet has.
A - tidestoneA long hexagon of clear crystal with chamfered edges; the crew glows inside it, thin cyan seams run along the cuts, brass claws close over both points, a lime seed crystal at one tip. Reads as something cut, not manufactured.
B - nautilusA dark lacquered chambered shell with brass septa; a pearl in its mouth throws the crew up as a standing sheet of light. Lying, it is the strangest object of the three; held, the sheet fills the view and the shell drops to its foot.
C - light scrollTwo long triangular prisms of hull metal with cyan trim and brass caps; the crew is a membrane of light stretched between them. The closest to the game's prisms; it unrolls rather than wakes.
Recommended: AIt is the one that stays itself when held: the crystal and its claws frame the crew on every side, where B's shell and C's prisms leave the frame when the view closes in. It also matches the converter's crystals, so the Ark's two crystal things belong together.
