The Meerkat's omnicrystal grows a tunnel network to every exit. Each junction comes after a straight so you can choose, the twists come right after the fork, and the tube rolls you with it like a waterslide. Every path is scored for comfort.

How the network is built

From the crystal the tube runs straight to the first junction. At each junction the exits still to reach are split by where they lie around the rider (right, up, left, down in the rider's own frame) into two to four groups, one per way out.

Each way out is three pieces. A split peels off toward its direction. A twist is a steered path. It never turns tighter than the tightest-turn radius, eases into and out of each turn over the turn ramp like a slide's clothoid, and loops round if it needs room to line up. A straight leads into the next junction, or into the exit lined up with the exit's position, facing and up.

The rider's frame is carried along without spin (a rotation-minimising frame) and then rolled. Banking turns the rider so a bend is felt as pitch rather than a sideways shove. Through a helix (a waterslide spiral in the tube's path right after the fork) the banking rolls the rider round with it, which is what makes a helix feel good rather than sickening. An exit's own roll eases in over the end of its run. No straight before a junction ever rolls.
