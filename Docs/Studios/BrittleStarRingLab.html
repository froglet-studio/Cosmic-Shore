Brittle Star Ring Spin wagon-wheel test bench

The right stick turns the five-point support ring. In the current build, spinning the stick faster than about 1.5 turns a second makes the ring stutter backward (the wagon-wheel look). Past 3 to 4 turns a second it freezes in place and jitters. All five scopes get the same stick on the same frame clock. The first is the control: the shipped code, line for line.

Why the control does it

BrittleStarExecutor.AimRing runs once per frame:

  1. NearestPoint picks whichever of the five turrets is closest to the stick's angle right now. They sit 72° apart, so the chosen turret is never more than 36° away.
  2. PhaseToAim + Mathf.MoveTowards turn the ring toward that turret at no more than ringTurnRate = 540°/s, which is 1.5 turns a second.
  3. Spin the stick faster and the ring falls behind. Once it is 36° behind, the next turret is nearer, and it sits behind the aim, so the ring turns backward to meet it. That is the stutter.
  4. The faster the stick, the more evenly the ring splits its time between forward and backward. At about 4 rev/s (60 fps) the two cancel: the ring sits still and jitters ~20 times a second, the static glitch. At 30 fps it can even settle into turning the wrong way.

The five turrets look alike, so a pentagon turned 72° looks the same. That is why the stutter reads as a wagon wheel rather than as a turret change.

What each fix would change in Unity

  • Keep turning forward: AimRing keeps the turret it committed to. While the stick sweeps, it only changes to the next turret ahead in the sweep direction, never one behind. Same 540°/s cap. About 20 lines in BrittleStarRules, no asset change.
  • Faster ring: keep the code and raise ringTurnRate. One number, but the same failure returns past the new limit. Near 2160°/s at 60 fps the pentagon moves exactly half a turret spacing per frame, which is true strobing.
  • Forward + motion smear: the forward rule with a faster cap, and the drawn ring smeared across the angle it swept that frame (a rotational blur on the ring's material, or ghost copies). Hides display aliasing at any speed. Needs shader or mesh work in BrittleStarAnimation.
  • Flywheel: the ring has its own spin speed. It matches the stick's sweep rate up to a cap, accelerates with limited torque, and settles onto the committed turret when the stick stops. The ring then reads as heavy rather than glued to the stick.

Check the control against the editor

  1. Set Game frame rate to what the editor or the phone runs at (the Stats overlay shows it).
  2. Pick Gamepad and spin the same pad's right stick (press a button first so the browser sees it), or use Ramp.
  3. The control should match the game: smooth up to ~1.5 rev/s, a backward stutter at 2, frozen jitter by 4. Turn on Fire to see the firing turret jump around.
  4. Use ¼× or Step to watch it frame by frame. The strip under each scope draws the ring's turn rate, red where it runs backward.

Not modelled: the camera's own motion, Creation Mode's corner trails, Charge Mode's cords and Space Mode's splay. They all hang off the same ring phase, so they inherit whichever rule wins here.
