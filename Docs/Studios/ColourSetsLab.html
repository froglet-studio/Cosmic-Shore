Dart Frog

Every domain is two pigments: a bright one for the prism's rim and a dark one, a different hue, for its face. Jade is the green-and-bronze frog. The other five domains follow the same rules.

The pigments in OKLab

Up is lighter, around is hue, outward is chroma. Light and shadow slide a paint along a ray from black, so each pigment is a cone segment. Drag to turn the view. The faint dots are the edge of what a screen can show.

Mint

The rules every colour set keeps

  1. The band. Pigments of different domains stay at least 0.05 apart in OKLab, about 2.5 just-noticeable differences, as in the six-family truth.
  2. Lighting-proof. Their directions from black stay at least 0.05 apart, so no light or shadow turns one domain's paint into another's. This is what ruled out a forest face beside a lime rim at first: dark green is shaded lime.
  3. Contrast. A domain's rim and face differ by at least 90° of hue and 0.12 of lightness.
  4. Identity. Every rim still reads as its own family in the six-family truth, so a Dart Frog Jade is still Jade.
