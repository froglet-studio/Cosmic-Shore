The rules every colour set keeps

Garrett, 2026-11-10: the six families "must be distinguishable and have rules that govern the territory of their regions in color space", and "it must be obvious to the player based on a connection between their look and their four letter name".

  1. The band. Pigments of different domains stay at least 0.05 apart in OKLab, about 2.5 just-noticeable differences.
  2. Lighting-proof. Their directions from black stay at least 0.05 apart, so no light or shadow turns one domain's paint into another's: dark green is shaded lime.
  3. Form. A domain's rim and face differ by at least 0.12 of lightness, so a prism reads as a solid.
  4. The name. Every rim reads as its own family in the six-family truth in every way the game shows it: its swatch, as light (the prism's edge) and as paint (the HUD and the vessel tails read the same numbers as sRGB, which turns an orange Gold into Fire).
  5. Communication (a set made for a vision only). As that viewer sees the colours the game paints, rims, faces, the HUD's rims and each rim against its face stay the set's margin apart. The set opens drawn as its viewer sees it.
  6. The voice. A face is most of a prism, so it says the domain's name or keeps quiet: it is its family's own colour, its rim's own paint (Photic), or quiet, wholly below lightness 0.40 where only Fire and Blue exist, so a dark face of any hue reads as night under the rim (Nocturne).
  7. Apart. As painted, every two rims stay at least 0.10 apart, as light and as paint.

Three styles add their own rules: Photic, one paint per domain; Nocturne, lanterns over quiet jewel faces; Nacre, soft faces of each family's own colour. A set made for a colour vision keeps rules 4 and 6 as its viewer sees them: its rims and faces must look like the game's own colours of their family as that viewer learned them, and rule 5 keeps its rims apart.
