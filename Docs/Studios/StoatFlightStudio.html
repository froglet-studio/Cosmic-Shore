    Dipole sling round 6

    Press: the black hole is laid ahead and to the side on the trigger's side, the white hole the same distance ahead on the other side; neither moves. Squeeze depth (read live; curve from Squeeze) sets the strength between a touch and buried (GM = 20,000 × strength, the game's unit) and the horizon between its two sizes; the white hole mirrors both and pushes with push × the pull, softened over twice its horizon. The hold radius is the circle on which the pull exactly bends you round at your speed, GM = v²(r − rs)²/r: heavier, wider. Pass within catches you × that circle, with it off your nose by at least catches once (or once you are inside the circle), and you are in its grip: the engine holds your speed and you settle onto the circle and go round for as long as you hold, up to longest hold. Outside it the pull only bends you. Let go: it lets go of you, and you leave on the tangent with a boost of speed × (a touch … buried) by the squeeze; the pair falls together and annihilates. Stepped at 4 ms; diving through the portal keeps only the speed you had before the dive.

    Field trajectory round 15 · lab-only

    No catch, no hold circle. The pair is only a field: the black hole's pull GM/(r − rs)² and the white hole's softened push, capped at the force ceiling. It bends your nose and speeds you up or slows you along it; the engine pulls that gravity speed back toward cruise at engine holds speed. The poles are fixed (pole strength, horizon, white hole push). Squeeze either trigger and the pair opens ahead of you with the poles together. Then the two triggers place them (each on the squeeze curve): the difference between the triggers pulls them apart sideways (up to sideways u for one full trigger, the black hole on the deeper trigger's side), and the sum pulls them apart lengthways (up to lengthways u with both full), the black hole always the nearer, so you shoot into the sink and out of the source. One trigger gives a slight diagonal; both full put the black hole dead ahead and the white hole further ahead on the same line. The separation is the dipole moment. Ease off and the poles come back together; let go of both and they meet and annihilate, so there is only ever one pair. The line is your path on your current inputs, drawn as evenly spaced dots (dot size, gap) from just ahead of the nose, run with the flight's own step: blue-grey while the poles leave it alone, lime as soon as they warp it at all (bend it by boost once … bend in total) or it goes through the wormhole. While it is lime you travel down it at loop boost ×: the boost runs your own flight faster (steering, engine and field together), so you reach each point of the line sooner and the line does not change. It stops where it would loop back onto itself. In this mode the Stoat turns at pitch & yaw in this mode × its shipped rate, so the pair does more of the turning. Force ceiling and fastest bend come from the Dipole tab.

    Birth & annihilation how a pair comes and goes

    Birth. A horizon's radius is proportional to the mass inside it (rs = 2GM/c²), so the black hole grows from a point as its mass arrives — its pull, shadow and lens with it. A white hole is a black hole run backwards, so it arrives the way it will leave, reversed: a light shell converging from far away onto its spot, its core lighting as the shell lands. Annihilation. When the two meet, the pair's mass leaves as a gravitational wave: a burst moving outward (at light speed in reality; slowed here so you can watch it), weaker with distance. It does not push things outward like a ripple in a pond — it stretches space one way and squeezes it the other, alternately, so prisms pulse and the view stretches as it passes you. A head-on meeting radiates most sideways to the line the holes closed along and nothing along it, and it ends in a fading ringdown. As its mass leaves, the black hole sinks back to a point; the white hole's light leaves as an outgoing shell. Real waves are far too faint to see; these are scaled up.
