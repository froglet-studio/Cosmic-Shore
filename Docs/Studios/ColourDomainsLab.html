Cosmic Shore · palette field guide · measured from OriginalColorSetSO

Six words for colour

Every hue in Cosmic Shore belongs to one of six families, and each family has one meaning. Ruby, Gold and Jade are the three domains (freedom, commerce, order). Blue is neutral and owns every grey. Lime means "collect or click this". Fire means danger. Between the families sit bands of colour nobody may use, so that no colour can be read as two things. Section 02 has a bench for reshaping the families and saving the result as a proposal.

02 · The regions in three dimensions

One rule, five colour spaces

Each dot is one screen colour from a grid over the sRGB cube. The regions are always measured in OKLab, then drawn in the space you pick, so you can see how a perceptual boundary bends inside RGB or HSV. The band's thickness is measured in OKLab, so only the OKLab view shows it as an even slab; the other views stretch it. Left-drag empty space to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. Drag a ringed anchor to move it through whichever space is showing; right-drag to erase colours from the family that owns them; click a dot to pick its colour for the bench below.

03 · Colour vision

Which families merge for colour-blind players

About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women see colour differently. Protans and deutans (red-green, the common forms) lose one of the three cone signals, tritans (blue-yellow, rare) lose another, achromats see only lightness. The simulation is Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009), applied in linear RGB; lower severity models the milder anomalous forms.

05 · Method

The anchors and the rule

Nearest anchor (the rule in the game today). A colour belongs to the family whose nearest anchor is closest in OKLab, provided it is at least the margin (0.05, about 2.5 just-noticeable differences) closer to that family than to any other. Otherwise it is in the forbidden band. Blue's distance is also the colour's chroma, because every grey is Blue's. This is the rule in Tools/Build/kite_mote_colors.py and Docs/VesselModels/COLOR.md §5b, with the anchors read from the live assets on 2026-10-30. Its regions can be any shape, and a family with several anchors can be pinched or bent.

Convex cells: the source of truth (31 Oct 2026). Every family is one convex cell, and the six cells fill OKLab with no gaps: each family has a score that rises in a straight line across OKLab, and a colour goes to the family that scores highest there. The band is the margin's full thickness, measured in OKLab (0.05 is about 2.5 just-noticeable differences), centred on each border, so it only ever lies between two families and is the same thickness everywhere. The truth is Garrett's “shaped by eye”: he reshaped the regions with the eraser, and its boundaries are frozen exactly as the page fitted them then. Every anchor is at its game value and rests inside its own family, clear of the band, except the engine ember face (#300A05), which is within one band of black, so no convex border can clear it. The repo copy is Docs/VesselModels/colour_domains.json, explained in COLOR_DOMAINS.md and checked against the game by Tools/Build/colour_domains.py --check. When you edit here, the page refits from those frozen boundaries: each family's anchors and taken colours sit as far from every border as they can, at least half a band away. Erasing hands a colour to the neighbour that scores highest there; a colour that can't change hands without opening a wider gap stays where it is. Reach raises a family's score so its borders slide toward its neighbours. Lime's extra green-yellow window (HSV hue 75–105°, no blue) applies only to the four element shades, so it is not drawn here; all four rest inside Lime.
