How the contour field bends light

Every pixel's ray is marched through space. Away from the tube it travels straight. Inside the shell its direction is turned by the field there, then it carries on to the sky, prisms and floor.

d distance to the wall (− inside, + outside) τ shell: inner × r inside, outer × r outside w = shape(1 − |d| / τ) 1 on the wall, 0 at τ T along the tube (the long grid lines) Φ round the tube (the rings) F = cos(pitch)·T + sin(pitch)·Φ k = strength / (½ · (inner + outer) · r) drag dD += k·w·(F − (F·D)·D)·ds twist dD += k·w·(D × F)·ds glass dD += Δn·∇(w)⊥·ds

Grazing scales k by mix(1, |D·n|, grazing). At 0 a ray skimming the wall bends most, as through real glass; riding inside the tube, every ray skims, so the ride view swirls. At 1 every crossing bends about the same.

T is the true surface tangent of the long lines (it tilts with the flare and with the bend), so a field line traced on the wall is a grid line at pitch 0° and a ring at 90°. Strength is roughly the bend, in degrees, of a ray crossing the wall head-on; grazing rays bend more, which is what gives the tube its outline.

In the game this would replace the mouth dents and the rim warp with one full-screen pass. It marches only the pixels the tube's screen bounds cover, with the same distance function the studio's grid uses (whAt, whRadius).
