Cosmic Shore · Voyages · gameplay design · 24 Nov 2026

Sail a sea that is alive

Voyages become a curated open world for a squad of three: you sail the real HyperSea map cell by cell, something is always happening next door, every vessel class has a job worth doing, pilots have opinions, and rival crews want the same prize. Then: what Milestone 2 builds, in order.

Where we are

Why it feels linear today

Read from the voyages branch, not from a playtest. Each point below is a code fact that produces the feeling you described.

The leg is a count, not the mapA job's leg is "N cells" (VoyageRaid.CellsFor), filled from a pool of open-sea worlds. The cells you fly are not the cells the map draws between the two places.
Next door is emptyOnly the cell the Ark is in runs. Neighbouring cells hold nothing you can see or reach, so there is never a reason to look around or change course.
One job, one verb, one voyageEach voyage is a single job resolved at one stop. Ten of the 31 job types resolve on arrival with nothing to do (missions audit, 16 Nov).
Any vessel fits any jobNothing on a job card or in the run asks for a kind of vessel, so the squad pick never matters and most of the fleet has no reason to sail.
Pilots are only a pair of eyesA pilot is a callsign sight plus a faction. They have no mood, no history and no say in what work they take.
Rivals live in a spreadsheetNine rival crews exist in voyage_slice.json, but they are resolved by a daily tick. You never meet one in the world during a run.
The map is a menu screenThe HyperSea map lives in the Voyage window. In flight there is no way to see where you are on it.
Three minutes with no turnsSalvage (was Crystal Run) is collect, survive, dock. There is no moment where the squad has to decide something together.
Five pillars

What a voyage promises

1 · The sea is real

You fly the map

The cells you cross are the cells on the HyperSea map between two places. Pause shows the map with your Ark on it, mid-lane.

2 · Next door is alive

Something is always happening

A director lights live events in the cells beside your course: a wreck surfacing, a bloom, a rival crew, a distress call. You can see them and detour.

3 · Every class has work

Voyages use the whole fleet

Vessels sort into seven classes. Each mission and each event wants certain classes, so the squad you bring changes how the voyage plays.

4 · Pilots are people

Mood and experience

Pilots come from zones, like some work and hate other work, and get better at what they do often. A reluctant pilot sails worse.

5 · Three minutes, three friends

One story per run

Every run has a start, a job, a turn and a landing, with a decision the squad makes together and a moment each player owns.

Rules that stay

What this never changes

A mode never changes a vessel's kit. PvP is petals only. Every colour comes from the colour set. One HUD layout on every device, current text sizes.

Pillar 1 and 2

The HyperSea, as you sail it

The galaxy is one shared, authored map (VOYAGES.md §3). A lane between two places crosses a run of cells. In Milestone 2 the voyage flies exactly those cells, in order, with each cell's density and danger taken from the map (HyperSeaCourse.Steps, already used by the escort's course). Five zones give the sea its character and decide which pilots and events you find there.

Celadon Reach (Jade home) Carmine Drift (Ruby home) Aurum Banks (Gold home) The Crossing (contested) The Deep (uncharted) your course live event
Traversal

How a run moves across the map

Places are nodes, lanes are cells. A run sails one lane: three cells for play (a short-leg answer to VOYAGE_BOARD.md §5 decision 8). The Ark enters each cell through its membrane, crosses it, and leaves toward the next one on the map. Arriving docks the Ark at a real place, which becomes the next run's start, so a session of four runs is a real journey you can trace.

A detour bends the course one cell sideways into a live event, then rejoins the lane. It costs time on the clock and gives the event's reward.

Pause shows the map

Pause, and you are on the chart

Pressing pause in a voyage opens the same HyperSea map the Voyage window draws (VoyageMapGraphic, the foam you shaped), framed on the course: the Ark's live position inside the current cell, the cells behind and ahead, live events beside the lane with their timers, rival crews in range, and the next dock. Pause menu buttons stay where they are; the map is the background panel.

In multiplayer the game keeps running while one player looks (a party never freezes), so the map is a quick read, not a menu.

Live events in neighbouring cells

A director keeps one or two events lit beside the course at any time and never lets a player go 20 seconds without something to do or look at. Each event is shown in 3D in the world (a light through the neighbouring membrane, per the LOCKED world-fact rule) and as a mark on the pause map. Each one uses work verbs and fauna the game already has (DEPTH.md §4).

Pillar 3

The fleet by class

Seven voyage classes, sorted from what each vessel's four abilities actually do (Docs/FleetCodex/abilities.json). A class is a label for voyages: missions and events ask for classes, and squads are judged by them. It never changes a kit. The escort AI already has roles for five of these (Sparrow destroyer, Squirrel cultivator, Dolphin blaster, Serpent and Scarab guardian); the names here grow from those. Garrett's Brood Rush will measure each vessel's cultivator/destroyer position; where that measurement disagrees, it wins.

voyage AI ready  has an escort role today (*Role.cs), so it can fill an empty seat. Other vessels can be flown by a person on a voyage but have no voyage AI yet.

Pillar 4

Pilots with opinions

The sixteen faction types already split into three domains (DEPTH.md §4). We group them into three guilds and give every zone of the sea its own mix, so where you dock decides who you can recruit and who gives you work.

Wardens · Jade

Keep things standing

Aristocrat, Priest, Researcher, Preservationist, First responder, Diplomat.

Love: hold, tend, rescue, observe. Hate: raze, killing near a nursery.

Raiders · Ruby

Take what is out there

Hunter, Pirate, Explorer, Activist, Thief.

Love: salvage, hunt, survey, liberate. Hate: waiting at a core, courier runs.

Traders · Gold

Make the sea pay

Merchant, Bounty hunter, Gatherer, Contractor, Sport.

Love: convoy, harvest, bounties, building. Hate: culling, anything with no pay.

Mood

How a pilot feels about this job

Each pilot reads the job card through their guild and faction: Keen (their kind of work), Steady, Reluctant (not their kind of work), or Refuses (against their ethos: a Preservationist on a cull, a Priest on a raze). A refusing pilot will not take the seat; you swap them or skip the job. Mood also moves with the voyage: a run that pays well or saves the Ark lifts it; losing the hold drops it.

Experience

Pilots get good at errands

Every completed job adds to the pilot's count for that work verb (Hold, Stow, Cull, Raze...). Three ranks per verb: Green, Seasoned (3 jobs), Veteran (8 jobs). A veteran salvager on a salvage run is the best seat you can bring. Shown as a small rank pip on the seat card.

How mood and experience change performance

Through the pilot, never through the vessel

The LOCKED rule says a mode never changes a kit, so a pilot never touches speed, damage or cooldowns. Instead:

When an AI flies the seat: the pilot sets how well the AI plays. Keen or Veteran plays as a stronger AI; Reluctant plays weaker and more cautious. This fits AI_PLAN.md, where the pilot already biases the seat's AI, and goes into Garrett's queue.
When a person flies the seat: the pilot's callsign sight (LB) is stronger: a Keen pilot's sight lasts longer and reaches further; a Reluctant one's flickers. Pay and the scoreboard credit the pilot's mood too.

Missions and the people who offer them

Givers who tell you what to bring

Each job card names its giver (a pilot from the zone you are docked in), what the work is, and the squad the giver recommends, by class. Bring that squad and the pay goes up; bring something else and the voyage still works, it is just harder. Your own pilots react to the card before you set sail. Try it: pick a mission, then change the three seats.

Rival crews

Someone else wants the same prize

Rival crews stop being a tick on a spreadsheet and sail into the run as three AI vessels in their domain, named on arrival and on the pause map. The nine crews in voyage_slice.json already have a faction, three hulls, a strength and a temper; the relation rules (co-op, race, hostile) already exist in VoyageRivals.Relation. No story arc, only named crews that behave like their temper and remember you.

Race

Same job, first wins

They want the same wreck or bloom. Whatever they stow is theirs. Hunters and Pathfinders cut them off.

Hostile

They want your hold

A Pirate with a tip on your laden Ark breaks Ark mass by the arena rules. Protectors and Guardians hold them off.

Co-op

Three more wings

Same domain: they fly escort for a share of the pay. A good run with them makes the next one friendlier.

Grudge and bounty

They remember

Beat a crew twice and they come looking for you. Notoriety 3 puts a bounty on your squad in every port.

Pillar 5

Three minutes that are fun with friends

Every run is built from the same five beats. The director fills them from the mission, the cells on the course and the events next door. Target: first action within 15 seconds, never more than 20 seconds of nothing, one squad decision, and each player credited for something on the scoreboard.

Split the work

Three jobs at once

Each beat asks for three things at the same time: guard the Ark, work the objective, deal with the event or rival. The class on each seat suggests who does what; nobody is told.

Shared eyes

Callsign sights become pings

When you hold LB, your squadmates see a faint echo of what your pilot lights. Three pilots, three sights, one squad that sees the sea. No new button.

One squad decision

The detour vote

When an event lights next door, a five-second vote on the D-pad: detour or press on. Ties go to the host. Solo, it is your call.

Clutch moments

Everyone sees the danger

Ark breach warnings, a rival grabbing the last salvage, the kaiju wake: shown to the whole squad in the world, so saves are shared moments.

Credit for everyone

A scoreboard of roles

End-of-voyage awards by class: Wall of the Voyage (most Ark damage stopped), Top Salvager, Rival Breaker, Herd Master. Every seat earns one line.

Keep the party together

Join at the next cell

A friend who joins mid-voyage drops in at the next membrane crossing into an empty seat, replacing its AI.

Milestone 2

Scope and the order to build it

In Milestone 2
  • Voyages fly the real map: 1 to 6 cells a lane, 3 to 5 minutes on average, real docks, pause shows the map
  • Neighbour-cell event director with 5 events: wreck, bloom, rival crew, distress call, predator pack
  • Seven vessel classes as data, on job cards, seat cards and the Fleet Codex
  • Four missions open: Escort the Ark, Salvage, Bounty, Hold the Core
  • Givers per zone, recommended squads, a squad-fit meter and pay bonus
  • Pilot mood (4 states) and experience (3 ranks per verb), applied to AI seats and callsign sight
  • Rival crews in the world: race, hostile and co-op
  • Multiplayer layer: shared sight echo, detour vote, role awards; a two-device party test
  • Voyage AI roles for three more vessels, so every class has an AI-ready vessel except Shepherd
Not in Milestone 2
  • Lair Hunt, Liberate the Pen and Courier Race missions (designed, stay locked)
  • Penned herd and kaiju wake events (kaiju appears only as a climax at difficulty 7–9 if time allows)
  • Player-versus-player rival squads, galactic traffic control, a networked Ark (V2/V3)
  • Pilot recruitment economy, bunks and promotions beyond mood and rank
  • Shepherd AI (Angler, Jacks, Starling, Jewel Wasp are flown by people only)
  • Sagas and relics, Episodes, the Saga tab
  • Any change to a vessel's kit, or a new HUD layout

Build order

Each step lands as its own PR into voyages and can be played in Unity on its own. Steps 2 and 3 come first because without them every mission still feels linear.

Done when

Real map

Four runs in a row trace a real path on the pause map, docking at real places.

Pacing

A 3-minute run has a first action by 0:15, no gap over 20 s, and at least one detour offered.

Choice matters

A recommended squad clears each mission at difficulty 4; a mismatched one clears it less than half the time.

Fun with friends

Three people on two or more devices finish a voyage and each gets a scoreboard award. You decide if it was fun.
