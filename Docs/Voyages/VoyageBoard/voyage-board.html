    Reading the chart

    • The galaxy is ours, unchanged. Every named star, cloud and nebula sits where astronomers put it. Zoom in and the sea resolves into cells, each 20 light years across and one minute of sailing.
    • Colour is the sea's density as your crew feels it. Expanded, they sweep so much gas that it reads as a liquid: thin spray in hot bubbles, water in the open disk, thick water in the arms, reefs in molecular clouds. Reefs are impassable, so courses bend round them.
    • Thick water is rich and rough. Denser gas grows more prisms, prisms feed fauna, fauna grow bolder. Spray is safe and empty. A voyage is as hard as its roughest water or its destination, whichever is harder; two backers ease it one step.
    • Couriers are the only fast news. Every voyage shows how long light would take. A message the Ark carries in twenty minutes would take light four hundred years.
    • The side view is the disk edge-on, at true scale. The galaxy is 1,000 ly thick, 50 cells, so from far out it is a hairline; zoomed in, the gauge on the left shows where in that thickness your Ark is. Clouds are thin sheets near the midplane, and above and below the disk the sea thins to the halo's spray.
    • Crew's eye switches distances to what your expanded crew perceives: a cell is 2.25 million km, the whole galaxy 75 AU.

    Decisions for Garrett

    1. Tumult sits 2,300 ly from the galactic centre, toward Sol. Home waters are calm spray inside Tumult's own bubble; the centre's reefs are two hours out. Any point 24,000 ly from Sol fits the canon.
    2. The Heart (Sagittarius A*) is closed. The deepest well squeezes an infused traveller past return. The other option is a late lair.
    3. Sol is closed. The Terminal guards Earth's well, so courses route round it. The Hyades beacon is a proposed Terminal harbour for a start near Sol.
    4. Sea density sets danger and reward. Reefs over 100 g/cm³ are impassable.
    5. Sol to the edge. The map uses the real 26,000 ly from the centre; Timelines' 15 hours to the edge would need 32,000.
    6. Reefs are routed round, not over. They are sheets a few hundred ly thick, so a course could climb over one through thin water. Allowing it would add a slow, safe third course.
    7. Voyages run 8 to 55 minutes (160 to 1,100 ly), so the galaxy is a lifetime and Earth is a pilgrimage.
