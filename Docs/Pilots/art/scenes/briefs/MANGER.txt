A documentary photograph taken in the field by a photojournalist who waited hours for it, in the manner of the best wildlife and expedition photography. A 400mm lens at f/4 from a long way off, the camera low, at the subject's own height; one blurred stem leaning through the near foreground, the subject sharp, the distance soft. Natural light only. The subject is at work and has not noticed the camera. The whole figure is in the frame, head to foot, a little over half the frame's height and off-centre, with the place filling the rest. True-to-life colour, a little grain, nothing retouched.

The subject is Tamako Deephook, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: ibis and platypus and human in one body. IBIS is the face and the shape of the head: a scarlet ibis's bare pink face, a long down-curved red bill, vivid scarlet feathers. PLATYPUS is the bill shield and everything behind the face: a broad flat platypus bill shield lying over the brow like the peak of a cap, soft dark blue-grey leathery skin rounded at the front with two nostril dots, and sleek dense brown platypus fur over the rest of the head; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all platypus, dense brown waterproof platypus fur, meeting the ibis's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are a person's, forward-facing and expressive with a little white showing at the corners, deep brown irises each ringed by a thin glowing ruby magenta line (the pilot's sight). No human hair anywhere on the head. In detail: the whole face is scarlet and the long bill rose-red, greyer toward its tip; the cape has a broad turned-down collar with magenta piping on every edge; the jerkin is dark brown, the line is a coil of pale hemp rope, and the strap's buckle is square brass.

From the neck down Tamako Deephook is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: lean and long-legged, a wader's build. Both hands are the platypus's own: each is a platypus's webbed forepaw, dark skin stretched between clawed fingers, and the wrist above it wears the platypus's own coat. Each hand has fingers and a thumb and holds things the way a person's hand does. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. The platypus also gives a broad flat furry platypus paddle tail raised behind one shoulder: seen whole, the paddle tail is as long as a forearm and a hand, grows from the base of the spine and comes out under the back of the cape.

Tamako Deephook's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a moss-green oilskin stalking cape over a laced leather jerkin, a coil of line across the chest, piped in ruby magenta (#C800C3), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly, and a leather feed-satchel strap across the chest, a few crystal grains caught in its buckle; below it, mud-brown canvas breeches and tall laced leather boots, resoled more than once. Both knees and shins are plastered with wet grey mud; the hem of the cape is soaked dark and spattered; a three-cornered tear near the hem has been mended with coarse pale stitches; the leather is cracked at its creases; the fur of the neck is damp and spiked; there is silt under the claws, and water stands in beads on the oilskin. Above the mud line the magenta piping still shows bright along the collar and down the front edges of the cape.

Tamako kneels on one knee at the edge of a shallow channel on a feeding flat and sets a lure. One webbed hand pays out wet line from the coil worn at the chest; the other lowers a small hand-carved wooden float with one glinting crystal bead into the water. The long bill is tipped down over the work and the eyes are fixed on the line. The paddle tail lies flat on the mud behind, as a brace.

The feeding flat lies inside a living cell the size of a valley: grey silt and mirror-still water a hand deep, stands of pale glassy reeds whose seed heads are clusters of small clear crystals, and far behind, through mist, the wall of the cell, a translucent sea-green membrane veined like a leaf and curving up out of sight. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. Soft, shadowless green-white daylight filtering down through the membrane, the mist brighter behind the figure, the wet mud shining.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt. The head and everything that grows from it are Tamako Deephook's own living anatomy, never a mask, a costume, a hat or a headdress. The eye rings and the piping glow no brighter than a reflection. One person only: nobody else is anywhere in the frame, near or far. Nothing in the frame carries writing, numbers or a symbol: every label, sign, crate and screen is blank. No weapon, no watermark, no border, no human ears.
