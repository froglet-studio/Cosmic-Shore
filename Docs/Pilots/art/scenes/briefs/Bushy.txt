A harvest reportage photograph at golden hour: a 50mm lens shooting toward the low sun, so the chaff and dust in the air burn gold and every spine has a rim of light, while the pale ground throws a soft light back into the face. Warm colour, fine grain, the moment caught mid-toss. The figure is seen from the knees up. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Bushy a third of the way in from the left edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Bushy, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: horned lizard and hedgehog and human in one body. Bushy's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a canvas harvest apron over a rolled-sleeve shirt of undyed linen open at the throat, a straw-coloured kerchief in the pocket, piped in amber gold (#FFA700), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly; below it, stained canvas dungarees and short rubber boots. Bushy stands on the threshing floor and winnows: both gloved hands hold a wide flat basket and have just tossed the seed up out of it, so the seed hangs in the air in a bright arc, hard and clear as glass beads, while the wind takes the chaff away in a long gold plume. Bushy's eyes are on three small brown birds that wait in a row on the wall for the spill, and the wide lizard mouth is curved in a slow smile: warm and kind. The face is turned three-quarters to the camera.

A round threshing floor of pale packed clay on a hilltop, ringed by a knee-high wall of dry stone: a wooden fork, a broom, a row of full sacks tied with twine. Behind the wall the crop still stands, stalks as tall as masts, each bowed under a single seed head the size of a rowing boat; three cut heads lie on the floor like upturned hulls. Beyond, stubble terraces step down into evening haze. The hill is empty of people. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. The sun is low and straight behind the floor: the plume of chaff burns gold, the spines and the horns are rimmed with fire, the falling seed sparkles, and the pale clay throws a soft warm light back up into the face.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt.
