An editorial portrait made for a feature about a replanting, the subject asked to hold still in the middle of the job and not pleased about it: an 85mm lens, flat bright overcast, the pale ground filling every shadow, a clean composed frame. Natural colour. The figure is seen from the waist up, standing among the planting. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Tender a third of the way in from the right edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Tender, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: parrot and narwhal and human in one body. PARROT is the face and the shape of the head: a scarlet macaw's face, a big curved bill, bare white cheek skin, scarlet feathers. NARWHAL is the tusk and everything behind the face: a narwhal's smooth domed brow of mottled grey-and-white whale skin over the top of the head, hairless and glossy, and one long straight spiral-grooved ivory tusk jutting forward and down from the left side of the upper jaw, the way a narwhal carries it; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all narwhal, mottled grey-and-white narwhal skin, speckled dark like a leopard seal, meeting the parrot's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are a person's, forward-facing and expressive with a little white showing at the corners, steel-grey irises each ringed by a thin glowing jade teal line (the pilot's sight). No human hair anywhere on the head. In detail: the face is scarlet with bare white cheek skin and a big curved bill, pale above and dark below; the top of the head is a narwhal's smooth domed brow of mottled grey-and-white skin, and the neck is the same, speckled dark; one long straight spiral-grooved ivory tusk juts forward and down from the left side of the upper jaw; the jacket is faded green with a strap of seed pouches across the chest.

The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. Tender's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a ranger's jacket of faded green with a seed-pouch strap across the chest, piped in jade teal (#0EC0B6), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly; below it, green canvas trousers, damp to the knee, and rubber wading boots. Tender has been asked to hold still in the middle of the job, and has, more or less. A seed case as hard as stone and the size of an apple is clamped in the big curved bill, half opened, its two halves parting along the seam and the pale seed showing. One green-gloved hand is cupped under it to catch the seed; the other holds a planting dibber. The head is cocked, and one steel-grey eye is turned on the photographer with the lid half down: wry and unimpressed. The face is turned three-quarters to the camera, both eyes open. The long ivory tusk points past the camera, and the grey fluke rises behind one shoulder.

A reef that has bleached and is being planted again, at low water: a wide flat of bone-white lattice growths, dead and brittle, standing in ankle-deep clear water over pale sand, and between them row on row of new plantings, each a green shoot no taller than a hand, tied to a thin cane. Beside Tender floats a wooden tray of seed cases with a sack and a bundle of canes on it. Far off, the living edge of the old reef rises like a blue-green cliff of lattice, with mist on it. The flat is empty of other people. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. Flat bright overcast: soft, even and shadowless, the scarlet feathers and the bare white cheek both holding detail, the pale sand and the white lattice filling every shadow from below. A dull silver glare lies on the water.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt.
