An architectural interior photograph made on a tripod with a shift lens: every vertical true, the room square to the frame, a long exposure at a small aperture so the room is sharp from the floor plates to the window bars, the one figure in it standing still. Cool, exact and unhurried, the colour muted. The figure is seen from the knees up, standing. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Ilia Silvergate a third of the way in from the left edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Ilia Silvergate, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: narwhal and seahorse and human in one body. NARWHAL is the face and the shape of the head: a narwhal's large rounded whale head with no neck, a blunt bulbous melon brow, mottled grey-and-white skin, and one long thick spiral-grooved ivory tusk jutting forward from the upper lip. SEAHORSE is the coronet and everything behind the face: a seahorse's bony coronet of coral-orange spikes on top of the head, with small translucent fan fins at the temples; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all seahorse, seahorse ring-plates, coral orange and gold bony segments, meeting the narwhal's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are the narwhal's own, small dark gentle whale eyes, each rimmed by a thin glowing jade teal line (the pilot's sight). No human hair anywhere on the head. In detail: the skin of the head is pale grey mottled with darker grey and black flecks, darkest over the brow and palest at the throat; the tusk is pale ivory and as long as the head, no longer; the coronet is a row of coral-orange spikes that runs from the brow over the top of the head and down the back of the neck like a crest; the jacket has a standing collar, leaf embroidery in dark teal thread down its front and a froth of white lace at the throat; the chain of office is silver links set with oval sea-green stones.

From the neck down Ilia Silvergate is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: tall, heavy and smooth, deep-chested, with a slow straight carriage. Both hands are a person's hands in gloves: pale grey doeskin gloves. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. The seahorse also gives a seahorse's ridged prehensile tail as long as a leg: seen whole, the seahorse's tail grows from the base of the spine and comes out under the hem of the clothes at the back.

Ilia Silvergate's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a midnight-blue velvet court jacket with a low lace collar and a jewelled chain of office, piped in jade teal (#0EC0B6), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly, and a compass-needle pendant on a fine chain at the throat; below it, pressed pale breeches and tall riding boots. The velvet is rubbed bare at the elbows and the right cuff shines with winch grease; the lace has gone limp in the damp; the gloves are dark at the palms; dried salt lies in the grooves of the tusk.

Ilia stands the last watch of the night at the gate. One gloved hand rests on the long brass lever of the gate winch and has not moved it; the other is held behind the back. The seahorse tail has come round from behind and taken two turns round the brass handrail at the hip, the way a seahorse anchors itself in a current. The great head is turned three-quarters to the camera and tipped toward the chart on the wall, the near eye fixed on the last ring of its course line: stern, watchful, waiting for someone who is late.

The gate room above a harbour's sea gate, before dawn: a tall narrow room of pale stone and dark iron. Beside Ilia stands the gate winch, a brass drum and lever worn bright, its chains running down through slots in the floor plates. On the wall beyond hangs a flat chart panel, a sheet of dark glass in a scuffed metal frame. It shows a sea chart of deep space seen from straight above: dark blue water dusted with fine white points, drifts of violet where it thickens, islands of coral orange with violet shores, one wide grey shoal inside a thin teal shoreline, small ring and diamond markers joined by thin pale lines, and one lime-yellow course line stepping from marker to marker. The panel is old and dim, no brighter than a lamp turned low, its glass smeared with fingerprints. The chart is colour, lines and plain marks only: no letter, number, label or icon anywhere on it. The panel is as tall as Ilia and twice as wide. To its side, one tall barred window looks down the harbour channel into blue-green haze; under the window, a bench, a folded blanket and a cold cup. The room is empty of people. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. The window is the main light, a cold grey-green before dawn that lies along the tusk, the brow and the spikes of the coronet. A caged work lamp over the winch makes a small warm pool on the lever and the glove. The chart is dimmer than either and puts only a faint blue on the near velvet sleeve.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt.
