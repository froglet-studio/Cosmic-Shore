A frame from a remote camera half buried in the sand beside a nest and tripped by movement: a wide lens almost at ground level and tilted up, a little sand on the bottom of the glass, hard afternoon light, everything sharp from the near grains to the sky. Plain colour. The figure is seen from the knees up, bending down toward the camera. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Strider a third of the way in from the left edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Strider, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: shoebill and crocodile and human in one body. SHOEBILL is the face and the shape of the head: a shoebill's face, a huge clog-shaped bill, pale horn-yellow mottled with grey and hooked at the tip, slate-blue feathers, a small tuft at the back of the head. CROCODILE is the scutes and everything behind the face: rows of square armoured crocodile scutes, olive-brown and keeled, covering the crown and running down the nape in a double ridge; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all crocodile, olive-brown armoured crocodile hide with a pale cream throat of small smooth scales, meeting the shoebill's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are the shoebill's own, pale yellow shoebill eyes with a hard unblinking stare under heavy brow ridges, each rimmed by a thin glowing ruby magenta line (the pilot's sight). No human hair anywhere on the head. In detail: the bill is huge and clog-shaped, pale horn-yellow mottled with grey and hooked at the tip; the feathers of the face are slate blue with a small tuft at the back of the head, and the eyes are pale yellow under heavy brow ridges; square olive-brown scutes cover the crown and run down the nape in a double ridge, and the throat is pale cream; the cape is moss-green oilskin over a laced leather jerkin.

From the neck down Strider is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: tall, gaunt and long-legged, standing very still. Both hands are a person's hands in gloves: worn russet leather field gloves. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. The crocodile also gives a heavy crocodile tail with a double row of ridged scutes, trailing on the ground behind: seen whole, the crocodile's tail grows from the base of the spine and comes out under the hem of the clothes at the back.

Strider's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a moss-green oilskin stalking cape over a laced leather jerkin, a coil of line across the chest, piped in ruby magenta (#C800C3), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly; below it, mud-brown canvas breeches and tall laced leather boots, resoled more than once. The oilskin cape is faded on the shoulders and caked with dry black sand at the hem; the lacing of the leather jerkin is half undone in the heat; the coil of line on the chest is green with river weed; the gloves are dark with water; the breeches are wet to the thigh, and the tall boots are grey with silt.

Strider has walked up from the river with the great bill full and is bending over the mound, tipping the bill so that the water runs from its hooked tip in a thin steady stream onto the warm sand over the eggs. One gloved hand is braced on a knee; the other steadies a carrying pole laid across the shoulders, with two empty water skins hanging from it. The pale eyes look down past the bill at the mound without blinking, under heavy brows: weary but steady. The face is turned three-quarters to the camera, both eyes open. The crocodile tail has drawn a curved furrow in the sand behind.

A hatchery beach of warm black sand beside a slow green river: a row of low mounds, each ringed by a little fence of woven reeds and marked with a plain stick, one of them freshly wetted and steaming a little. In the nearest mound the sand has slipped, and the curved tops of three leathery eggs show, each the size of a melon and faintly translucent. Behind stand reed beds and a hide of bundled reeds, and far over the river, trees as tall as towers with pale trunks, hung with long moss. The beach is empty of other people. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. Hard afternoon sun from high behind the camera: every scale and feather edge sharp, the stream of water lit like glass, a deep shadow under the oilskin cape. Light thrown up from the wet sand fills the underside of the great bill and the pale throat.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt. The head and everything that grows from it are Strider's own living anatomy, never a mask, a costume, a hat or a headdress. The eye rings and the piping glow no brighter than a reflection. One person only: nobody else is anywhere in the frame, near or far. Nothing in the frame carries writing, numbers or a symbol: every label, sign, crate and screen is blank. No weapon, no watermark, no border, no human ears.
