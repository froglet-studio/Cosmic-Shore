A long view down the nave of a chapel, from a tripod: a 135mm lens that presses the rows of stalls close behind the figure, the light from candle points and one high window, the exposure long and exact. Warm pearl colour, fine grain. The figure is seen from the knees up, standing in the choir. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Maestro a third of the way in from the right edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Maestro, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: platypus and butterfly and human in one body. PLATYPUS is the face and the shape of the head: a platypus's face: a broad, flat, soft dark-grey duck bill with two nostril dots near its tip, and sleek dense brown fur. BUTTERFLY is the antennae and everything behind the face: two slender black butterfly antennae with clubbed tips rising from the brow; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all butterfly, orange butterfly-wing scales with black veins, meeting the platypus's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are a person's, forward-facing and expressive with a little white showing at the corners, deep brown irises each ringed by a thin glowing jade teal line (the pilot's sight). Human hair: long honey-brown human waves swept to one side, leaving the antennae clear. In detail: the bill is broad, flat and dark grey, and the fur is sleek brown; two slender black antennae with clubbed tips rise from the brow, and the neck is orange veined in black like a monarch's wing; the honey-brown hair falls in long waves over one shoulder; the robe is deep indigo edged in teal over a dark flight suit, and the pendant is one white shell.

From the neck down Maestro is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: broad and low, thick through the body, with a slow rolling walk. Both hands are the platypus's own: each is a platypus's webbed forepaw, dark skin stretched between clawed fingers, and the wrist above it wears the platypus's own coat. Each hand has fingers and a thumb and holds things the way a person's hand does. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. The butterfly also gives two great monarch butterfly wings, orange veined in black and edged with white dots, growing from the back between the shoulder blades and standing open behind both shoulders, behind the clothes and well clear of the head and the neck: seen whole, the butterfly's wings grow from the shoulder blades, pass through two slits in the back of the clothes and fold down the back to the hips.

Maestro's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a long layered robe of deep indigo over a flight suit, a shell pendant at the chest, piped in jade teal (#0EC0B6), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly; below it, a long plain under-robe to the ankle and sandals. The indigo robe is worn pale at the elbows, and its hem is grey with soot and dotted with spilled wax; one sleeve is pushed back to the elbow; the shell pendant has swung round on its cord; the sandals are scuffed; a little orange wing dust lies on one shoulder.

Maestro stands alone at the music desk with a steel tuning fork held up beside the head in one webbed forepaw, just struck and still ringing, and is humming the note back at it, the broad bill a little open. The other forepaw is raised with one clawed finger up, as if to hold the fork to account. Maestro eyes the fork sidelong with one brow raised, having decided that the fork is the one that is flat: cool and assessing. The face is turned three-quarters to the camera, both eyes open. The monarch wings stand half open behind the shoulders.

The choir of a chapel built inside nested shells: vault within vault of pearly ribs rising out of sight, the mother-of-pearl catching a hundred candle points. Two rows of dark wooden stalls face each other down the nave, every seat empty, a closed plain book on each ledge. Before Maestro stands a slanted music desk with a book of blank ruled pages held open by a smooth stone, a candle snuffer and a second tuning fork. A shallow channel of still water runs down the middle of the floor and doubles the candles. The chapel is empty of people. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. Candle points are the main light: warm, small and many, soft on the brown fur and the grey bill, bright along the edges of the orange wings. One high window adds a thin cool shaft across the music desk and the raised fork. The pearl vault behind glows without detail.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt. The head and everything that grows from it are Maestro's own living anatomy, never a mask, a costume, a hat or a headdress. The eye rings and the piping glow no brighter than a reflection. One person only: nobody else is anywhere in the frame, near or far. Nothing in the frame carries writing, numbers or a symbol: every label, sign, crate and screen is blank. No weapon, no watermark, no border, no human ears.
