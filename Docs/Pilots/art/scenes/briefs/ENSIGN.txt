A garden portrait on a medium-format camera held at waist level: an 80mm lens, slow fine-grained colour film, first sun, long soft shadows. The sitter knows the photographer is there and has not moved for it. The whole seated figure is in the frame and fills most of its height. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Veliane Crestward a third of the way in from the left edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Veliane Crestward, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: crane and lemur and human in one body. CRANE is the face and the shape of the head: a whooping crane's white face, a long pointed yellow bill, a bare red crown patch, black whisker marks. LEMUR is the ears and everything behind the face: two round fluffy white lemur ears edged in black, the only ears on the head; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all lemur, soft grey lemur fur with a white throat, meeting the crane's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are a person's, forward-facing and expressive with a little white showing at the corners, warm hazel irises each ringed by a thin glowing jade teal line (the pilot's sight). Human hair: short spiky white human hair, leaving the ears clear. In detail: the bill is pale yellow and as long as the head, and a black stripe runs back along each cheek; the lemur ears stand out at each side of the head, and a tuft of the short white hair stands between them; the coat has teal piping on its collar and cuffs, and the sash is jade teal silk.

From the neck down Veliane Crestward is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: tall and slender, long-necked and long-legged, with a dancer's straight back. Both hands are the lemur's own: each is a lemur's slender black leathery hand with long thin fingers, and the wrist above it wears the lemur's own coat. Each hand has fingers and a thumb and holds things the way a person's hand does. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. The lemur also gives a long lemur tail ringed black and white, raised behind the head and curled over one shoulder: seen whole, the lemur's tail grows from the base of the spine and comes out under the hem of the clothes at the back.

Veliane Crestward's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a tailored coat of ivory brocade with polished buttons, its collar folded open at the throat, piped in jade teal (#0EC0B6), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly, and a small forked pennant badge on the shoulder and a sash across the chest in the accent colour; below it, pressed pale breeches and tall riding boots. Wet lime greys the cuffs of the ivory coat and one knee of the breeches; the sash is knotted out of the way; the boots are dull with dew and stone dust; a smear of lime has dried on the bill.

Veliane sits in the one gap of the sea wall, where a stone is still missing, the way a lemur sits to take the sun: upright, knees apart, the slender black hands open on the knees and the chest turned to the first warmth. The ringed tail is wrapped over one shoulder like a stole. The stone that will close the gap waits beside one boot, with a trowel and a bucket of wet lime. The eyes are half closed against the light but open, the long bill level, the face shy and gentle and for once at ease, turned three-quarters to the camera.

The seaward edge of a formal water garden at sunrise: a long low wall of white stone, crusted with salt and lichen, running away beside still mirror pools and clipped silver-and-lilac hedges. Beyond the wall there is no horizon: pale water goes out into soft blue-green haze, and far out a few pale faceted crystals hang above it, turning slowly. Dew lies on everything. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. The first sun comes low from the sea side, warm on the white face, the bill and the black palms, and rims the round ears and the ringed tail. The garden behind is still in cool shadow.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt. The head and everything that grows from it are Veliane Crestward's own living anatomy, never a mask, a costume, a hat or a headdress. The eye rings and the piping glow no brighter than a reflection. One person only: nobody else is anywhere in the frame, near or far. Nothing in the frame carries writing, numbers or a symbol: every label, sign, crate and screen is blank. No weapon, no watermark, no border, no human ears.
