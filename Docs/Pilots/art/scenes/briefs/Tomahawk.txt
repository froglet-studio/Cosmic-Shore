The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Tomahawk a third of the way in from the left edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Tomahawk, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: basilisk and hawk and human in one body. BASILISK is the face and the shape of the head: a green basilisk lizard's face, a tall sail-like crest on the back of the head, bright green scales. HAWK is the mantle and everything behind the face: a mantle of streaked brown-and-cream red-tailed hawk feathers sweeping back from behind the face, over the nape and down the neck, leaving the top of the head bare of feathers; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all hawk, cream hawk breast feathers streaked with dark brown, meeting the basilisk's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are the basilisk's own, round golden basilisk eyes with a round dark pupil, each rimmed by a thin glowing ruby magenta line (the pilot's sight). No human hair anywhere on the head. In detail: the face is bright leaf green with a pale throat, and the sail crest stands tall and ribbed on the back of the head; the hawk feathers of the neck are cream streaked with brown, and the tail feathers are rust red.

From the neck down Tomahawk is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: light, lean and quick, long in the leg, always about to move. Both hands are a person's hands in gloves: worn russet leather field gloves. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them. The hawk also gives a red-tailed hawk's tail feathers, rust-red with a dark band near the tips, fanned out level behind the hips, and barred hawk wings half open behind the shoulders: seen whole, the hawk's tail grows from the base of the spine and streams out behind the hips, and the wings grow from the shoulder blades through two slits in the vest.

Tomahawk's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a quilted grey tracker's vest over a rough cream wool shirt open at the throat, a bone toggle on a cord, piped in ruby magenta (#C800C3), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly; below it, mud-brown canvas breeches and tall laced leather boots, resoled more than once. The grey vest is dark with spray down one side and has dust caked white in its quilting; the cream shirt sleeves are wet to the elbow and cling; the breeches are soaked to the thigh and powdered pale; the bone toggle has swung back over one shoulder; a few wing feathers are beaded with water.

Tomahawk is running across the lagoon on the water itself, as a basilisk can, the body leaning forward and the arms swinging, the barred hawk wings half open for balance and the rust-red tail fanned out behind like a rudder. Each footfall punches a dark ring through the lagoon's skin of pale dust and throws up a crown of spray, and a line of those crowns runs back out of the frame behind. Beside the runner a dark seam in the dust shows where something large swam under it minutes ago; one gloved hand points down at the seam in mid-stride. Tomahawk has glanced over at the camera, the golden eyes round, the wide mouth a little open and the head tipped: bright, open and curious, as if to ask whether the camera sees it too. The face is turned three-quarters to the camera, both eyes open.

A lagoon in the floor of an old crater at first light: black water under a skin of ash-grey crystal dust as fine as flour, just strong enough to take a light runner for one step. The seam, a wandering dark line of open water as wide as a doorway, runs on ahead toward a far shore of black cinder slopes, where one vent glows dull red and a thread of smoke stands straight up. Tall dead trunks of glassy stone stand in the lagoon. The sky is clear and pale gold at the rim, and the lagoon is empty of other people. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. The sun is just over the crater rim behind the runner's shoulder: it rims the green crest, the cream feathers and the open wings in gold and lights every spray crown from behind, while the pale dust throws soft light back up into the face. The far shore is a dark streak.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt.
