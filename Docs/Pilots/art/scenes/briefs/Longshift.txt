An expedition photograph taken in a blizzard with a compact camera in a gloved hand: a 28mm lens, the exposure set for the black fur, so the white ground burns out almost to paper. High-key, pale on pale, low contrast everywhere but the figure. Wind-blown crystal dust streaks flat across the frame and a few grains have stuck to the lens. The subject is sitting the weather out and has forgotten the camera. The whole seated figure is in the frame and fills most of its height. The frame is wide, sixteen by nine, with Longshift a third of the way in from the right edge and the place filling the rest. Clear space above all that grows from the head. The head is sharp and lit, the whole face easy to read, the place behind it soft.

The subject is Longshift, a real living person in a world where people like this are ordinary: black hare and badger and human in one body. BLACK HARE is the face and the shape of the head: a black hare's long face, sleek black fur, a white muzzle with a cleft upper lip, and two very long ears standing taller than the head. BADGER is the stripes and everything behind the face: the badger's head pattern over the whole top of the head, a white cap from the brow to the nape split by two bold black stripes that start above each eye and run back over the crown, three white bands and two black; the back of the head, the nape, the throat and the neck down into the collar are all badger, coarse grizzled silver-grey badger fur, black at the throat, meeting the black hare's face along the jawline and the temples in a soft natural blend. The eyes are a person's, forward-facing and expressive with a little white showing at the corners, violet-blue irises each ringed by a thin glowing ruby magenta line (the pilot's sight). No human hair anywhere on the head. In detail: the head is black, and the three white bands are narrow: one runs up the middle of the forehead from between the eyes and one above each eye, all three passing back between the ears; each ear is as long as the head, black outside and dusky pink-grey inside; the parka is padded sky-blue cloth with magenta piping along the zip and the pocket flaps, the straps of a pack lie over both shoulders, and the compass is brass, the size of a palm, on a leather cord.

From the neck down Longshift is built like a person and stands, sits and moves like one: lean and long-legged, light on the feet, quick to startle. Both hands are the badger's own: each is a badger's broad dark forepaw, with five toes and one long pale digging claw on each toe, and the wrist above it wears the badger's own coat. Each hand has fingers and a thumb and holds things the way a person's hand does. The clothes cover the body from the collar to the wrists and the ankles, and no bare human skin shows between them.

Longshift's clothes, seen whole and lived in: a padded sky-blue survey parka worn unzipped with the hood pushed back, a brass compass on a lanyard, piped in ruby magenta (#C800C3), the piping a thin cord that glows faintly; below it, patched canvas trousers with a mended knee and dusty trail boots. The parka is crusted white with rime down its windward side, one pocket flap is torn half off and pinned shut, the canvas knees are dark with wet, one bootlace has been replaced with cord, and fine crystal dust has gathered in the fur of the neck and along the stripes.

Longshift sits with folded legs in the lee of a boulder, sitting out the blizzard, with a field chart panel propped against the pack at one knee, its glass turned toward the camera. One long pale claw rests on the glass at the far amber ring, out at the end of the last arm of the chart, and stays there. Longshift is not looking at the chart but out over the top of it into the white, with a small private smile, shy and gentle. One ear stands straight up; the other is turned toward the wind. The face is turned three-quarters to the camera, and rime has gathered on the whiskers.

A hollow among pale boulders on a slope of white crystal scree, in a blizzard of crystal dust. Against the pack stands a flat chart panel, a sheet of dark glass in a scuffed metal frame. It shows a whole spiral galaxy seen from above, drawn as a chart of a sea: long arms of soft sea blue flecked with violet wind out from a short pale-peach bar at the centre, the gulfs between them nearly black, small gold points scattered round it; one small amber ring near the centre, another far out along a spiral arm, and a thin lime-yellow line between them in short straight legs. The panel is old and dim, no brighter than a lamp turned low, its glass smeared with fingerprints. The chart is colour, lines and plain marks only: no letter, number, label or icon anywhere on it. It is a field panel no bigger than a tea tray, its frame bound in cloth tape. Beside it, a tin cup, a spirit stove gone out and a coil of white line. Behind the boulders the slope climbs into white-green fog, where one huge pale round shape stands, more guessed than seen. A few tiny faceted crystal grains drift in the air like dust in a sunbeam. Flat white light from everywhere at once, with no shadows. The only colours are the blue of the parka, its magenta piping and the chart, whose dim blue and violet are the darkest things in the frame after the fur. The chart lights nothing.

All of it is photographed as it is, with a real camera: living anatomy with the fur, feathers, hide and scales of real animals, every hair resolved, wet where it is wet; real cloth, leather and metal with weight and wear; real dirt. The head and everything that grows from it are Longshift's own living anatomy, never a mask, a costume, a hat or a headdress. The eye rings and the piping glow no brighter than a reflection. One person only: nobody else is anywhere in the frame, near or far. Nothing in the frame carries writing, numbers or a symbol: every label, sign and crate is blank, and the chart is the only screen that is lit. No weapon, no watermark, no border, no human ears.
