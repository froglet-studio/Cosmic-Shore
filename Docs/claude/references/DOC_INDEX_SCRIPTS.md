| `SERPENT_SNIPER_SCOPE.md` | `_Scripts/Controller/Vessel/R_VesselActions/` | The Serpent's **scope** (Space, LT, analog zoom on the trigger's own depth) and **sniper shot** (Charge, RT while scoped). **Read before adding a camera VANTAGE, before magnifying the view from any ability, or before writing a second force that breaks super-shielded mass.** It records: **the magnification is a SECOND PICTURE, never the flight view** — a round eyepiece drawn beside it while the pilot keeps flying — because the first cut took the gameplay camera into the cockpit and narrowed its FOV and read as *nauseating*, and **a magnified view is a lever on every motion that reaches it** (the pilot's turn, the hull's roll, the camera's settle and the speed tunnel's own narrowing all multiply by the same ~4x as the target); so **the zoom composes with NOTHING but its own trigger** — the two terms removed to get there were each defensible, one a design idea (a stick-driven 'Steady Eye' bleed, whose Space-5 upgrade existed only to switch it off and was re-cut to **Deep Focus**, one more magnification step) and one a genuinely correct composition (the speed tunnel going on narrowing the scoped base), which is why 'compose magnification with nothing' is the rule rather than 'do not compose it with the stick'. That inversion DELETED `VesselFirstPersonView` and `CustomCameraController.FirstPerson`/`FirstPersonOffset`, and left the speed-tunnel home-FOV override (`Docs/SPEED_TUNNEL.md §2.1`) standing with NO caller as a guard rather than a feature — a direct `Camera.fieldOfView` write is still baked in permanently. and — from its SECOND playtest — **an indicator whose only rendering is its VALUE has no rendering at its extremes, and its extremes are usually the two readings the player actually needs**: the recharge arc was a bare fill, so it drew NOTHING on the frame the shot fired (`Sweep01 = 0`) and nothing once it was ready, which is indistinguishable from having no readout at all — answered with the goal stack's own rule (`Docs/GAME_MODE_TOPBAR.md`, *a progress bar needs a BED*): a dim full ring the fill runs over, and READY drawn as a complete bright ring rather than as the absence of one; and the scope's window is built on that preview shape rather than as a revival of the retired `Pip` prefab, whose dangling border texture draws a navy quad over ~55% of the screen, with its cost (a genuine SECOND render of the world, only while the trigger is held) stated rather than hidden. **From its THIRD playtest came a fleet-wide UI fix**: `AbilityLockupView.SetAbilityCooldown` refused a LOCKED card, so this vessel's recharge — correctly driven from the day the ability shipped — landed nowhere for three rounds, because the Serpent binds 0/4 icons; Two hardenings rode along, each a failure that would have presented identically: a ZERO hull measurement is no longer latched (`MeasureCircumscribedRadius` skips disabled renderers, so a hull asked before its art is on answers 0 and a latched 0 parks the eye inside the ship for that vessel's whole life — it re-asks while the answer is 0, warns once by name, and floors the eye in FRONT of the hull), and the render target is `Create()`d outright because the surface binds it in the same frame. **Its FIFTH playtest reported the same four words a round after a fix designed to make them impossible, and the answer was to stop guessing**: since round 4 retired the cockpit, the eyepiece is the ability's ONLY visible output, so ONE report — "it is not there" — covers four unrelated faults (never engaged / returned at a gate / ticked but invisible / drew and was covered), and three rounds were spent picking between them from source. `SniperScopeDiagnostics` is the house answer (`MouseFlightDiagnostics`' shape): **a refusal is an unconditional warning naming the gate** and **the happy path is one line on a channel that is off by default** — so enabling `CSLogChannel.SerpentScope` is the whole procedure for separating the first two cases from the last two, and there is deliberately NO rung for "it is being covered", because that is not decidable from source and an unconditional "everything checks out" warning becomes a permanent false positive the day the real defect is fixed (the guidance rides the happy-path line and names **Report On-Screen UI** instead). Two real defects were found reading for it, and the first is fleet-general: **`Canvas.enabled` and `GameObject.SetActive` are not interchangeable ways to hide a generated UI — only one of them lets its graphics rebuild afterwards.** A `Graphic` caches its canvas in `m_Canvas` and `IsActive()` is `base.IsActive() && m_Canvas != null`, so every `SetVerticesDirty` is a silent no-op while that cache is null — and `OnCanvasHierarchyChanged` nulls the cache and THEN tests `IsActive()`, which is false *because it just nulled it*, so it never re-caches in either direction. Hiding by the canvas therefore froze the reticle at its first-frame zoom and the arc at its first-frame sweep (the rim and backing survive on the pending `OnEnable` rebuild, which is what makes it look like a working instrument until you watch it move); `SelfCheck` asserts it through `IsActive()` and never through the `canvas` PROPERTY, whose getter re-caches on read and would heal the thing being tested. The second: **`IVesselStatus.ShipTransform` is `Vessel.Transform` with no guard** and `VesselStatus.Vessel` logs an error and returns null, so it THROWS rather than answering null and takes the whole instrument with it — resolved through a cached `ResolveHull` that falls back to the executor's own root. Everything else was measured clean off the shipped assets (both executors present AND in `ActionExecutorRegistry._executors`, `_shipInstance` wired, `Input: 2` intact, the tracer colour never black), and the registry row is the one to keep: `Get<T>` falls back to `GetComponentInChildren(true)`, so an executor missing from that serialized list would still `Engage` and still zoom while `_registry` — assigned only in `Initialize` — stayed null and took `_shot` and the entire overlay with it. **An ability that half-works is not evidence that its wiring is complete.** Its stated limitation is that the cooldown's element scaling is OWNER-LOCAL — element levels never replicate, so a non-owner enforces only a FLOOR, which can never refuse a shot the owner admitted; the exact fix is to replicate the resolved cooldown the way `NetEchoSightShape` does. **Its SIXTH report was the same four words again, and the pilot supplied the answer the source could not**: *"you were showing the pip fine when you had the vessel view in the pip ... there has been no pip since we have tried to swap those."* The window was on screen for rounds 1-3; round 4 did not re-point it, it REPLACED it — one change swapped the surface (`RawImage` -> a generated circular `MaskableGraphic`, 16:9 -> square, pivot and hierarchy with it) AND re-pointed the camera. **When one change replaces a surface and re-points it, the report cannot tell you which half broke** — "I don't see it" is the same sentence either way — so rounds 5 and 6 each found a REAL defect (URP camera defaults; a `Canvas.enabled` visibility toggle that freezes UGUI mesh rebuilds) and neither changed anything on screen, because neither was about the surface. Its corollary, which the Scarab records from the other direction: **three correct fixes that buy no part of one complaint means the thing being fixed is not the thing that is broken.** Round 7 restores round 3's surface verbatim — the same component, the same 16:9, the same half-screen height, the same 16/220 margins — and keeps only round 4's camera; `ScopeDiscGraphic` is DELETED (nothing else referenced it) so two windows cannot compete for the next report, and **why it never rendered is recorded as unknown rather than as diagnosed** — the geometry, winding, UVs and rect arithmetic all read correct, so the honest entry is that the pilot asked for the window back, not for an explanation. Two things are kept from round 4 because they answer real problems rather than being part of the substitution: the reticle stays INSIDE the panel (the middle of the screen is no longer the scope, so a reticle there would measure a view nobody is looking through) projected against half the panel's HEIGHT, and the opaque backing stays, so a panel pointed at empty space is still visibly a panel. **Round 8 re-shaped that window into the CHARGE petal — one petal of the element flower — and the method is round 7's rule applied to a re-shape**: `ScopePetalImage` SUBCLASSES `RawImage` and overrides ONLY `OnPopulateMesh`, so the texture property, the white-texture fallback, the material handling and the whole rebuild path are inherited and the only thing that changed is the vertex list. **When a component renders and its shape is wrong, subclass it and override the geometry — never write a new one**: a bad emit then reads as a wrong SHAPE, which a pilot can report, rather than as an absence, which they cannot. The outline is a MEASUREMENT of the shipped `charge_petal` sprite's alpha rather than a drawing (`ScopePetalGeometry`), and two facts came OUT of the trace rather than being put in — the two lower edges meet at **72.30°**, the same 72° the five-petal flower is built from, and the shape is symmetric to half a pixel. Three things generalise. **Positions ARE the UVs** when an outline is normalised over its own bounding box, which is what lets a shaped window sample a render target with nothing squashed and nothing cropped — the shape IS the crop — and is why the target went square (the petal's bbox is square to within 0.5%, and `fieldOfView` is vertical either way so the reticle's projection is unchanged). **A shaped window cannot bound its own furniture by its bounding box**: every radius drawn inside it is capped by the petal's measured INRADIUS (0.59× the half-side, set by the two long edges running down to the apex, not the top edge), derived in a static constructor so re-tracing the sprite moves the picture, the frame, the reticle cap and the recharge ring at once. And **a corner sharper than a right angle needs a MITER, not a bisector** — the 72° apex's miter is 1.70, so a bisector-offset feather would be 41% too thin there and the point would read as harder-edged than the rest of the outline. The BACKING is the same component with no texture, which is what makes the frame the petal's own outline rather than a rectangle behind it. One thing was deliberately NOT done: the recharge ring stays a circle INSIDE the petal rather than a band sweeping its perimeter, because a circumscribing ring needs 1.18× the half-side and pushes the instrument off the left of the screen at this window's authored margins, and a perimeter band is a SECOND new generated graphic — this window gets one change at a time. **Round 9 gave it a SECOND reticle, over the FLIGHT view, and the pair is ONE function** — `ReticlePixels(coneHalfAngle, tan(fov/2), halfHeight, floor)` handed each view's own half-height and own field of view, so *both circles are the same measurement through different optics*: the eyepiece's **GROWS ~8x** across the zoom dial because its field of view narrows while the cone does not, and the flight view's **stays ~5 px** because the gameplay camera's field of view is not the scope's dial (it does widen a little under the speed tunnel, which is the same measurement correctly following its own optics). Only the FLOOR differs and it belongs to the CALLER, because the two views want opposite ones — 6 px keeps the eyepiece's ring a ring at the wide end, while the flight view takes 3 px since it is SUPPOSED to be small and a generous floor there would inflate the very number the ring exists to state. **The flight reticle is PROJECTED at the point the shot reaches, never centred**, and that is not belt-and-braces: the camera is on the shot's axis only while it is exactly behind the hull, which the Serpent's authored `(0, 0, -250)` gives in the steady state and its own smoothing takes away through every turn — so a centred circle would mark the CAMERA rather than the round, exactly when the pilot is swinging onto a target. It is stood DOWN when the projection's `z` is non-positive (the rear view), because **`WorldToScreenPoint` behind the camera returns a MIRRORED position — a plausible-looking lie rather than an obvious failure**. Both reticles are built with the instrument and hidden with it, so a mark on screen can only ever mean *I am aiming*; the cone itself is untouched and every round 3–8 number is byte-for-byte unchanged. Its new diagnostic rung (`NoGameCamera`) stands the flight reticle down and leaves the eyepiece working, which is why it is its own reason rather than a refusal of the whole instrument — reporting it as "the scope window is not drawn" would send the next reader to the wrong half. |

| `ONE_THUMB_MOUSE_CONTROLS.md` | `_Scripts/Controller/IO/` | The desktop mouse + keyboard scheme for the ONE-THUMB vessels (`SingleStickMouseInputStrategy`) — why a single-stick hull gets nothing from the dual-WASD mix, the **two-regime** stick model (`MouseVirtualStick`) — near centre a spring makes **mouse speed mean turn rate** (`deflection = drag px/s × unitsPerPixel / springPerSecond`, exact at any frame rate) and letting go straightens the vessel; out past `holdOuterRadius` the spring is exactly ZERO, so a sustained sweep parks the stick in the **hold annulus** and the vessel keeps turning with the mouse dead still (holding hard over: **228 px** against the pure spring's 917 for 3 s, and unlike the spring it does not grow with the turn). Committing costs TIME as well as distance (`holdEngageSeconds`), because hard over is only ~91 px at a responsive gain, so every ordinary flick saturates the stick and a position-only annulus latched on the first one and locked the vessel into a spin. `MouseFlightWidget` draws the stick as a centred reticle with that annulus as a ring, because the two regimes fly completely differently and nothing else on screen tells them apart. Also the pad-mirrored button map and why the shift keys are kept beside the mouse buttons, and why `XDiff` publishes the NEUTRAL 0.5 rather than full throttle. Carries two defects worth not repeating — a spring that only ran while the mouse was STILL (so any drag pinned the stick and no partial turn existed), and a dead zone applied to the ACCUMULATOR, which is a ratchet that blocked slow aiming movement outright and scaled with frame rate — plus the standing finding that **Invert Y is dead on every one-thumb vessel on gamepad, keyboard and touch**, because `DualStickMix` inverts only `YSum`/`YDiff` and those hulls read neither. §4.0 is the one that generalises past this scheme: **"which device is the player using" must have ONE implementation** — `InputDeviceIconSetSwitcher` detected by last meaningful ACTUATION while `InputController.SelectStrategy` keyed on PRESENCE (`Gamepad.current != null`), so a controller left plugged in gave the player ability chips that correctly named their keyboard and a ship that ignored it, with unplugging the pad as the only fix. Neither component was wrong alone; *the defect was that one question had two answers*, and no test can catch a second detector by calling the first — both now read `InputDeviceActuation` and `InputDeviceUnificationTests` holds it as a source law. **That fix was half a fix, and the other half cost five playtests**: the shared detector counted buttons, keys and clicks but NOT mouse movement, so a desktop player with a pad merely plugged in still could not fly with the mouse — `DetectInitial` hands a connected pad the family, a click won it back until the pad's resting stick crossed 0.25 (**drift qualifies**), movement was not evidence of anything, and every hand-over runs `ResetStrategyState` → `stick = Vector2.zero` so the mouse could not accumulate a deflection even on the frames it owned. Two changes close it: **sustained mouse MOTION actuates** (`MouseMotionActuation`, ≥120 px/s held 0.08 s — a jolt of one or two frames is a bumped desk, not a player), and the stick threshold is **asymmetric** (`StickThresholdFor`: 0.25 normally, **0.6 to take the family from an active mouse player**), because a stick off centre is the one signal here that worn hardware produces on its own. General shape: *unambiguous acts (a press, sustained motion) must outrank ambiguous held states*, and *taking a device back is a separate guarantee from taking it* — fix only the second and the handover is one-way. §4.3 records the roster and the reason it is a LAW: `IsSingleStickControls` is written by whichever transformer `TryGetComponent` resolves, i.e. by COMPONENT ORDER, so Falcon and Shrike carrying a second (inert, disabled) base `VesselTransformer` made them correct-by-luck rather than correct — both are excised and `OneThumbVesselCoverageTests` holds one-transformer-per-vessel. §4.1.1: **Escape is the OVERVIEW gesture** (`OverviewGesture`, Escape or pad Start), and each HUD answers it by invoking its OWN volume/pause button rather than reimplementing the overview, so the key cannot drift from the button; fullscreen moved off Escape to **F11**. A mouse pilot is therefore never handed the cursor mid-flight — the one control they needed it for is on that key, and the cursor is released only when the overview actually opens (both paths pause the input controller). §4.2 records why the scheme reports its own non-engagement (`MouseFlightDiagnostics`): when it declines, `KeyboardInputStrategy` still steers the hull off WASD and still fires every ability off the same keys, so "not engaged" and "broken" are indistinguishable on screen — *when a system's failure mode is that another system quietly covers for it, the system has to say so itself.* §6 is a SEPARATE, older bug it surfaced: **no vessel has ever shown a keyboard control chip**, because `InputHintBindingMap.BindingFor` answers a keyboard query with `KeyLeftShift`/`KeyRightShift` while `Resources/ControlGlyphSet` authors the label on the PAD row (one entry deliberately carries both representations) — now bridged by `InputHintBindingMap.Canonical` — and because the map had no keyboard control for the pad FACE buttons at all, leaving every such ability (the Sparrow's Mass and Time) permanently blank. The action keys are **QWER + Space**, assigned in reverse priority (the two mouse buttons are the highest, so only Space and R are needed), and `KeyboardInputStrategy` was moved onto the same keys rather than left on B/N — `ControlGlyphSetSO` authors ONE `keyboardLabel` per control, so two desktop schemes binding different keys makes the chip confidently wrong for one of them. **Read before touching any `IInputStrategy`, `DualStickMix`, `InputDeviceType`, `InputHintBindingMap`, or `ControlGlyphSetSO`.** |
