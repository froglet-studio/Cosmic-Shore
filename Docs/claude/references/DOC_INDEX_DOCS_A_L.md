| `ELEMENTAL_ECONOMY.md` | `Docs/` | **A vessel debuff MOVES elements; it does not decay them.** Every elemental crystal is one size (`ElementalCrystalSetSO.CrystalWorldScale`) and worth exactly one petal (§1, 2026-10-26). Every vessel-on-vessel elemental debuff is now a permanent TRANSFER out of the victim's base level, landing in exactly one of three places: **stolen** by the attacker (contact verbs — the Squirrel's joust, the Rhino's sword), **ejected** as free-for-all crystals launched from the victim's hull at the impact velocity (every ranged verb), or **burned** — destroyed, by a **hostile danger prism and nothing else**, which is the economy's only sink. Lifeform reproduction and spawning are the only source, so a match is pilots trading one pool of material. **The unit is the PETAL** — one integer level, one step of the HUD flower, and one crystal at world scale 1, which is an arithmetic identity three files have to keep (`PetalNormalized` x `PetalCrystalWorldScale` x `levelPerUnitScale`) and which `check_elemental_economy.py` asserts. Two rules make it conserve and both live on the VICTIM so no call site can get them wrong: **nothing partial ever leaves** (a hit accrues; a petal comes off only when the accrual reaches a whole one — which is why a 90-rounds-per-second gun mints one crystal per magazine rather than a speck per bullet, and why the flower's step and the crystal are the same event), and **you cannot take what is not there** (clamped to what the victim holds above level 0, so an attacker can never be handed a petal that did not exist). CONTROL: the Sparrow's guns shrank a **Rhino's** blade to 0.7x for 3 s (`VesselChangeSkimmerSizeBySparrowFullAutoProjectileEffect`), the Rhino's sword muted a victim's `RightStickAction` for 5 s (`VesselDamageBySkimmerEffect`) and froze their trail spawner for 10 s (`VesselPrismSpawnerCooldownBySkimmerEffect`), and a Rhino or Squirrel crystal blast muted it for 3 s (`VesselChangeSpeedByExplosionEffect` — misnamed; it changed no speed); `ShieldSkimmerScaleConfigSO.ApplyMaxSizeDebuff` went with them. | `ABILITY_LOCKUP.md` | `Docs/` | **The ability lockup (TOTEM)** — the one system that fuses a vessel's ability icon with the element indicator that upgrades it: **TWO BORDERLESS TRAPEZOIDS** meeting at their wide edges across a small gap (element flower in the upper, ability icon in the lower), the card (not the icon) carrying the upgrade as a bloom behind both plates plus a lift in their fill, the control chip below. **The gap is the divider and the silhouette is the frame** — both the hairline and the rim are retired, because a line and an outline were drawing a boundary the shape states on its own; two trapezoids facing wide-edge to wide-edge read as one waisted object where two borderless rectangles read as a list. **The two plates are the SAME HEIGHT and the two marks are the SAME SIZE** (a true mirror about the gap): unequal plates stack into a lopsided hexagon that reads as a coffin, and against a mirrored plate a smaller flower reads as an under-filled plate rather than as hierarchy — the two marks are different KINDS of thing in fixed columns, so size was never what told them apart (`PlateImbalance` ≤25%, `0.75 ≤ flower/icon ≤ 1.0`). A **graded band rides the two SLOPED sides only** — solid the whole length of each slant, then WRAPPING around both corners onto the horizontals where it grades to nothing (a grade that died on the slant left the corner unlit, which reads as unfinished), with **antialiasing baked into the geometry** as a zero-alpha feather either side, because a canvas gives a generated diagonal none. It never closes, so it is not the rim returning; the plate's core stays transparent behind it. Generated plates cost ~200 verts/card and are the TUNABLE form — the finalization path is baking them to PNGs (`Docs/ABILITY_LOCKUP.md` § Finalizing), where `Mask` reads the same sprite's alpha for the clips. The plates are GENERATED (`TrapezoidGraphic`), never sprited: a trapezoid has no 9-slice, so a sprited one would freeze the slant into the art and need one asset per direction — generated, the slant is a single float both plates read *mirrored*, and `bloomSprite` is the only authored asset left. The flower is the SHIPPED radial one, unchanged and drawn smaller than the icon; **no authored rect moves** (the card is a sibling behind the icon, the upper cell is added above), so a vessel adopts the style by gaining ONE component and no art. Fleet-wide and **structural** — ensured in `VesselHUDController.Initialize`, audited by FrogletTools > Vessels > Audits > Audit Ability Lockups, asserted by `AbilityLockupStyleTests`. **The lockup owns the ROW**: position, pitch, cell size, host scale and icon size all come from one style asset and are written onto every vessel, so a prefab cannot make the row diverge and no icon is ever re-authored to match (each icon's scale is DERIVED as `iconBoxSize / its authored size`). It retired eight things it superseded — the upgrade corner badge, the upgrade icon tint, the decagon button plate, the per-vessel RING gauges, the circular press glow, the Squirrel's undriven heat halo, the hairline divider, and the rim itself (with the `LockupPlate`/`LockupPlateRim` art). **The row is ALWAYS four cards**: a slot the vessel has no ability for yet renders LOCKED (quieter plate, hairline mark, no gauge track — never a padlock, because the ability is not locked to the *player*, it does not exist yet), which is what puts the Rhino on the fleet's UI instead of leaving it on the old one until design lands. **A locked card DOES draw the cooldown veil**, and that is the one exception in the whole style: locked is a fact about the ART, while a slot being DRIVEN is an ability that exists — the veil never needed an icon anyway, since it sizes itself on the ability PLATE. The Serpent proved it, binding 0/4 icons and reporting "no cooldown indication" for three rounds of playtest against a completely correct driver, so the rule is **an indicator that refuses to draw because its decoration is missing is indistinguishable from an indicator nobody is driving**; `SetUpgraded` is deliberately NOT changed by the same argument read the other way, because on a locked card there is nothing to light. **The fleet's gauge is a LINEAR fill that takes over the ability plate, never a ring** — a vessel binds its existing meter `Image` as `AbilityIconBinding.gauge` and keeps writing `fillAmount` on the very same object, so no drive site changes; the lockup only re-homes and re-forms it, MASKING it to the trapezoid rather than reshaping it (a `Filled` Image is a rectangle and would overhang a tapering plate; mirroring its value onto a `TrapezoidGraphic` instead would need a per-frame poll of somebody else's field). That re-homing is load-bearing, because a meter is regularly authored under a DIFFERENT ability's button than the ability it reports on (the Squirrel's boost fill under its skimming button, the Scarab's ball energy under its throttle button) — which is also why `Build` adopts EVERY gauge before retiring ANY chrome. **The fleet's COOLDOWN is a CLOCKWISE radial veil swept over the ability plate, OVER the icon** (`VesselHUDView.SetAbilityCooldown(element, remaining01)`; clockwise needs `fillClockwise = FALSE`, because the veil depletes — the flag names the direction the wedge is drawn and the moving edge is its far end) — radial where the gauge is linear and in front where the gauge is behind, so one card can carry both without the two reading as one meter running backwards. It is a VALUE the vessel pushes, not an `Image` it binds (a cooldown has no per-vessel artwork worth preserving), the overlay is built LAZILY so an ability that never recharges costs nothing, and it is the one piece of the lockup that lives OUTSIDE the card — it must darken the icon, and the icon is a later sibling than the card.
