| `PALETTE.md` | `Docs/` | The domain colour set (`SO_ColorSet`): which asset is live, what `_DarkColor`/`_BrightColor` actually are (prism **base face** vs **fresnel rim** — "Outside/Inside" is a misnomer), the **linear-HDR colour-space rule** (Rec.709/CIELAB apply directly; scaling a pair changes brightness but NOT contrast), the measured shielded-tier contract (ΔL\* 29.34 across all domains), and the **danger tier** — which has no colour fields of its own: it composes the domain's *shielded* base face with the shared domain-independent `EnvironmentColors.Danger` rim. **The invariant that outranks every per-tier contract (§4.0): in every tier, on every domain, the rim is brighter than the base** — it held on nine of twelve tier×domain pairs by accident rather than by rule, and each of the three violations was separately rationalised as a local trade-off before being recognised as one defect (danger was inverted on all three domains at ΔL\* −3.8; it is now +9.30). Two warm-hue traps are recorded there and are the reason those violations were invisible: **absolute `C*` is not comparable across hues** (equal chroma leaves a warm hue's blue channel starved, so judge by screen saturation *after clipping*, §4.1), and **authoring a peak channel silently overshoots `L*` at a warm hue** — set lightness against the other tiers first, then solve the channels. §2.2 covers the **crystal tier**: crystal colour signals WHO MAY COLLECT (element is shape, never colour), a free pickup wears the lime CTA, and in every crystal shader the composition is `lerp(dull, bright, (1−N·V)⁴)` — so at that fresnel power **`DarkCTA` paints ~93% of the crystal and `BrightCTA` is a 2.5% hairline**; tune the body, not the rim. **§2.5 is the caveat that costs you if you miss it: that is a statement about a CRYSTAL** — anything that does not compose the pair (a prism, a UI chip) painted from `DarkCTA` alone renders the shipped (0.28, 0.50, 0.08) as a dark olive rather than lime, so read `SO_ColorSet.GetCtaSignalColor()` (the sibling of `GetDomainSignalColor`, normalised the same way, alpha 0 when the palette authors no CTA). **§2.6 is the same trap a THIRD time and the one that generalises it: a colour authored FOR A SHADER is not a colour a UI slot may read.** `EnvironmentColors.Danger` is HDR at 1.498 **with alpha 0**, so a raw read both clips and is fully transparent — and a transparent tint is indistinguishable from a tint that never ran. Read `SO_ColorSet.GetDangerSignalColor()`, the third `*SignalColor` sibling. The rule for all three: the shader composes the pair, tolerates HDR and never looks at alpha; a HUD does none of those, so add an accessor rather than reading the field. It also records the finding that outranks §3 in practice: **gameplay bloom is CLAMPED at 0.5** (URP's default is 65472), so bloom saturates at max channel 0.5 and 56 of the 86 authored colours bloom identically — brightness above the clamp is a **dead dial**, and §3's "channels above 1.0 bloom" is false as shipped. Within the clamp, extra bloom is bought with bright **area**, not intensity. **§4.3** generalises §4.1 past prism tiers and adds the composition law: **two saturated OPPOSITE hues cannot be blended, only separated** — any lerp between them spends its range in a third hue that belongs to neither (and on an additive surface it also SUMS with what is behind it), so a two-colour effect reading as one muddy colour wants a threshold, not a new pair; plus the two judging rules that caught it — **ACES desaturates anything bright**, so "desaturated" is a post-tonemap measurement and the inspector swatch lies, and **judge a candidate at the size it will be judged**, because a downsampled contact sheet averages two colours into the third one. **Read before editing any `*BlockColor` or `*CTA` field, changing which field feeds a prism or crystal material, authoring a two-colour VFX, or trying to make anything glow harder.** |
| `WEEKLY_CHALLENGE.md` | `Docs/` | **The weekly challenge** — one curated objective per UTC week, the same one for every player. The rule that makes it work: **the definition is DERIVED, only the progress is STORED** — this week's challenge is `hash(UTC Monday) % pool` over an authored `WeeklyChallengeCatalogSO`, so it resolves on a cold launch, offline, and identically on every platform with no server round trip, while UGS Cloud Save carries only what genuinely differs per player (best value, completed, attempts). The hash is hand-written FNV-1a rather than `System.Random`, which is deterministic only *within one runtime's implementation* — not a promise two platforms can hold each other to. **The week starts on the UTC MONDAY** (ISO-8601; a boundary that is a matter of taste makes two neighbours draw different challenges for one day in seven), and the trap it guards is that **`DayOfWeek` numbers Sunday 0**, so the naive subtraction puts Sunday at the start of the FOLLOWING week. **A SECOND trap of that class shipped, in the FORMATTER rather than the boundary, and was found from outside this system** (the analytics wave cohort, which reuses this week key): `ToString("yyyy-MM-dd")` renders through **`CurrentCulture`, whose CALENDAR is not always Gregorian** — and Unity takes CurrentCulture from the device locale, so the same instant keyed as `2026-09-07` on en-US came out `1448-03-25` on ar-SA, `2569-09-07` on th-TH and `1405-06-16` on fa-IR. Those players drew a different challenge from everyone around them and never rolled over in step. All three period formatters now pass `CultureInfo.InvariantCulture`. General rule: **a date rendered without an explicit culture is a date in whatever calendar the device chose** — any KEY, id or filename built from a `DateTime` needs InvariantCulture, not just a displayed one, and the defect is invisible on every dev machine in a Gregorian locale. **Rollover is a period-key comparison, never a timer** (a scheduled callback is wrong every time the device slept through it or the clock moved). The challenge is an EXISTING arcade mode launched through the EXISTING arcade modal — never a second launch pipeline. **Two separate decisions that are easy to conflate: the ASK is fixed, the MODE is not altered.** The launch panel PINS intensity, domain and seat count and removes Add AI (plus: briefing = the objective, objective box hidden, preview look-only) because the challenge is one fixed ask with one attempt, not a lobby — while the RUN itself plays at **that mode's own end conditions**, untouched. A run-scoped `EndConditionOverridesSO.SetRunOverride` that made a challenge a *smaller* version of its mode is RETIRED and should not be re-derived; the panel's locks are NOT, and the natural reading of "the run is an ordinary match" — that the card should therefore be configurable — is the mistake to avoid. What survives from the retired mechanism is the authoring rule underneath: **an objective above what a match of that mode can produce is unreachable by construction** (Crystal Capture ends at 20, so "collect 30" never completes), and it should sit meaningfully BELOW that target because **a metric must be credited PER PLAYER** while an end condition is a domain SUM (which is also why Nucleus Rush, crediting a domain's *representative*, is out of the pool). Reordering the pool re-rolls which week draws which mode. Authored ONLY through **FrogletTools > Game Modes > Weekly Challenge**, which exists for the VALIDATION rather than the fields; its layout is **master/detail behind three tabs**, because N entries × 9 fields is a page and a half of scrolling to compare two numbers 400px apart. **The CHALLENGE is weekly, the ATTEMPT is daily — one run per UTC day, spent at LAUNCH** (the one ordering that survives an alt-F4 halfway through a bad run); the day boundary is a key comparison like the week's, a completed challenge is still playable tomorrow, and only a FASTER completion submits (`BestTimeMs`). Test mode (forced entry, a 'week' of N minutes, no attempt limit, a scaled clock) is inert outside the editor and dev builds AND fails a non-development build outright — a runtime gate makes a stray flag harmless, the build guard makes it loud. **The LEADERBOARD is "who completed this week's objective fastest"** — every challenge is *reach N of something*, so the only thing left to rank is the TIME, lowest first (`WeeklyChallengeLeaderboardService`, `WeeklyChallengeRanking`, `WeeklyChallengeLeaderboardPanel`). **Only a COMPLETION earns an entry** — a player who never reached the target has no time, not a slow one, and a sentinel would either rank non-finishers above finishers or bury the real times, leaving the end-of-week reward tiers computed off a list that is mostly not a ranking; `FinishAttempt` is the single site that can submit. It is **ONE leaderboard reset weekly by UGS, never one per week** (the SDK cannot create leaderboards, so a per-week id needs a server job minting them forever), and its **archive on reset** is what the separate reward system reads — rewards are deliberately not implemented here. **Three settings live on the UGS SERVICE and no code can enforce them** — the client SDK has no reset or delete call at all, so they are set in the dashboard, the UGS CLI or the Leaderboards Admin API and nowhere else: Sort Order **ascending** (the score is a time), update strategy **keep best**, and a **recurring weekly reset with archiving on**, aligned to the same UTC Monday. **TWO of the three fail silently and both are checked at runtime.** The sort order is the first — a wrongly-sorted board has real rows, real names and real times, simply upside down with the slowest run at rank 1 — so it is checked instead (UGS returns rows in rank order, so a correct board is non-decreasing) and screams once per session. **The RESET is the second, and it is the worse one**: the score is a time and the sort is ascending, so an old fast run outranks every new one **forever** and a board that stops resetting freezes on the week it stopped — which is how it was reported (*"not getting cleaned after the week reset"*). The net under it is that **every submitted score is stamped with the PERIOD it was run for** (`WeeklyChallengeRanking.PeriodKey`, metadata `"w"`) and every read keeps only the current one, so last week's rows go away on the client whether or not the service-side reset fired. Five decisions travel with the stamp: it is the **DRAW** key, deliberately not `WeeklyChallenge.PeriodKey` — that is the RECORD key and carries `attemptResetToken`, which re-issues the SAME challenge to hand an attempt back, so stamping it would orphan every score already submitted that week and fire the not-resetting error on a healthy board; a row that **does not say** which week it is from counts as NOT this week (unknown has to fall on the stale side, since an unstamped entry is exactly what a non-resetting board is full of — the stated cost being that every pre-stamp entry disappears from the panel); an unknown **CURRENT** period keeps everything, the opposite call for the opposite reason, since emptying a board over a missing catalog is worse than showing it; ranks are renumbered **only when something was dropped**, so a healthy board keeps the true world ranks UGS gave it and a filtered one does not read as 1st, 4th, 812th (the Friends scope's own argument); and the submit's metadata **stopped being optional** — it used to be null when the player had no avatar, and an unstamped row is one this game then hides from itself. **It is a net, not a fix**: a read can hide stale rows, never remove them, so `FetchPageAsync` pages past other periods for at most 5 requests (a healthy board pages **once**, asking for exactly the rows the caller wanted — byte-for-byte the pre-filter behaviour) and past that the panel reads short, while `WarnIfBoardNotResetting` asks for the real fix once per session. The **KEEP BEST** trap is the second reason the reset is mandatory: "best" spans the whole board's life, so once it is missing a player whose stale time beats their new one keeps the stale row and gets no entry for this week at all, which no client-side rule can undo. A code-side `BIG - time` encoding was rejected: it makes every raw score in the dashboard, every export and the reward archive unreadable to save one dashboard setting. Two implementation notes worth carrying: **`WeeklyChallengeRanking` is a project type, not the UGS entry** (a view taking `Unity.Services.Leaderboards.Models.LeaderboardEntry` drags the SDK into the UI layer, and the project already has TWO types called `LeaderboardEntry`); and **mm:ss.cc converts the whole value to centiseconds in ONE step and ROUNDS** — the obvious whole-seconds-then-`floor((s - whole) * 100)` prints 47.3 as `0:47.29`, because the double nearest 47.3 is a hair below it and the subtraction keeps the error. **No avatar travels with an entry** (UGS holds an id, a name, a rank and a score, not a profile), so the panel does not reference `SO_ProfileIconList` and rows keep the template's art; real faces need a second lookup and are a follow-up. The inert PlayFab-era `DailyChallengeSystem` / `DailyChallengeModal` / `DailyChallengeLeaderboardView` cluster deliberately KEEPS its old name: renaming a dead feature to match a live one is how two systems come to look like one. Do not wire both. It also now owns its OWN `DailyChallenge` struct (mode + intensity, nothing else) — the rename took the shared one and the dead system stopped compiling, which is the useful half of the discovery: the two features were sharing a value type, so "the legacy cluster is separate" was true of its names and not of its data. **Renaming a feature is a compile-time test of whether it was ever actually separate from the one it superseded** — what the compiler names is where they were still joined. |

So adding a shot type is a question about COVERAGE before it is a question about taste, and the three additions that are not azimuth gaps each fill a different axis: `Underside Pass` (the only view of a hull from below — Top Down's mirror in every sense, same free azimuth, opposite elevation, declining the mark for the same reason), `Dutch Pass` (the only real tilt, whose roll range deliberately does NOT span zero, since a range through level rolls the shot back upright in the middle of its own draw and stops being a dutch — the Sidecar split, one axis over) and `Telephoto Isolation` (the only long lens still inside the vision band's near edge, capped at 150 for the same reason Static Tracking Cam is). And **lead room is measured in FRAMES, never in metres** — `aimLeadSeconds × speed` is a world distance, so the 0.35 s that frames a 110u tracking shot is 105u of lead on a 300 u/s vessel and throws the ship clean out of an 8u chase camera's frame; the solve clamps it to a fraction of the shot's own distance. That one was found by the test suite rather than by reading the code, and it generalises: *a composition parameter expressed in units of the SUBJECT is unbounded relative to the FRAME.* **The TWO-SHOT is one geometric fact rather than a search**: when two vessels are 10-30u apart a capture has an 85% chance of framing BOTH, and the set of points equidistant from A and B is the perpendicular bisector PLANE of the segment joining them — so putting the camera anywhere on that plane and aiming at the midpoint yields equal distance (equal apparent size), a broadside pair at equal depth (the separation is the plane's normal, hence perpendicular to the optical axis) and symmetry about frame centre, all three together and exactly (measured: 8.9e-15 relative equidistance error over 200k random pairs). `elevationDegrees` and `aimLeadSeconds` are IGNORED by that solve and that is structural — the first pushes the camera off the plane, which is the one move that breaks equidistance, and the second slides both ships toward one edge; ignoring them beats authoring zeros, because **a promise you can author your way out of is not a promise** and this one fails silently (the photograph still comes out, just not as claimed). Candidates come from `VesselVisionShading.CollectStampedVessels`, the vision band's own roster — the same argument as reading the corridor's target, since **a platform law maintains the handle because the law depends on it being right** — which also excludes the toy matrices' mini hulls by construction. It meets three platform laws and changes only one — it takes the prism occlusion corridor's `SetSuppressed` hold for the capture frame (the corridor's second sanctioned holder, same argument as the replay camera's), leaves the speed tunnel and the vessel vision band alone, and shapes the shipped concept distances around the vision band instead: every concept sits inside the ~150u near edge where a hull still renders as itself, except one that breaks it deliberately and says so in its name — which is also what capped the 1.5x zoom-out applied to the whole solo library (Static Tracking Cam at 150 rather than its scaled 165, Establishing left alone at 220-520): *a ratio applied to a list of numbers is not a decision until you check what each number was up against.* **Each solo band is now a UNION rather than a window** (Over the Shoulder 12-39, Sidecar 14-51, Oncoming 18-67.5, Low Chase 8-27, Top Down 28-105, Static Tracking Cam 35-150), because that 1.5x scale moved each band's NEAR edge out along with its far one and so deleted the close shots instead of adding to them — a band running from the tight cut's floor to the roomy cut's ceiling gets its variety from the roll rather than from a decision made once at authoring time, and a test holds the vision-band cap by reading the edge off the SHIPPED asset rather than the C# field initializer. **Two things about the file itself are worth stating rather than reading off the word "4K"**: the WIDTH is a function of the window (`captureHeight` 2160 sets the height and the width follows the window's aspect, so 3840x2160 only on 16:9 — deliberate, since it makes a capture frame exactly what is on screen), and **MSAA had to be asked for** — `RenderTexture.GetTemporary`'s `antiAliasing` parameter defaults to **1**, so this shipped rendering every capture with no MSAA while the game beside it ran the URP asset's 4x, and it was not obvious precisely because post-process AA *was* correctly adopted: the shots were anti-aliased, just less than the screen they came from, and stair-stepping on a prism edge reads as "the renderer" rather than as a missing argument. It now requests `QualitySettings.antiAliasing` snapped to a legal 1/2/4/8 and RESOLVES it with a blit, because a multisampled target cannot be `ReadPixels`'d directly. *A default-valued optional parameter is a decision nobody made, and a quality default of "none" fails by looking slightly worse rather than by failing.* **The SHIPS in a capture are MARKED**, by a scoped `VesselVisionShading.BeginCapturePass` that does two things for the one hand-stepped render: it lifts the local-pilot exclusion (a photograph OF that hull is the one case the exclusion's reasoning — *do not clutter a pilot's own cockpit view* — does not cover, and without it the feature is empty since the subject of a solo shot is always the local ship), and it COLLAPSES the band onto one flat THRESHOLD (`markDistance` **100**) — past it a hull is the solid domain-coloured silhouette, inside it the hull renders as itself, with nothing in between and the centre break-up closed at the same point (leave the authored 900 and every marked photograph comes back an outline with a dithered middle). At 100 the mark is a thing the PULLED-BACK shots do and the close ones do not — eleven of the fifteen concepts can never reach it — so **check what each concept's range was up against before moving the number**, the same rule the 1.5x zoom scale records. A concept may also **DECLINE** the mark (`ScreenshotConcept.markVessels`: Top Down, Underside Pass and Static Tracking Cam do), and that is a VETO rather than the per-concept band it lives beside — it leaves the rule statable in one sentence (*past `markDistance`, marked, unless this shot said not to*) where a per-concept band replaced the one sentence with fifteen. Static Tracking Cam is the case it was added for: at 35-150 against a 100u mark the same named shot came back a hull about half the time and a silhouette the other half, which is the very inconsistency the flat threshold was adopted to remove, arriving through the DISTANCE roll instead of through the concept. The band's four control points and two graded edges exist because *a mark that pops on reads as a new object appearing*, and **a photograph is ONE frame**, so there is nothing for a fade to protect against — dropping the edges is free here. An earlier pass rescaled the law's whole three-beat arc onto EACH CONCEPT's own zoom range; it was correct, it kept every shape ratio of the law, and it made "will this ship come back a silhouette?" a question you could only answer by knowing which of eleven concepts the roll landed on: *a rule you cannot state in one sentence is one nobody can aim.* The FAR edges are deliberately NOT moved — they exist so a pilot is not reading coloured dots across half an arena, which a photograph never does. It is **not the suppression hold that law deliberately does not have**: a suppression can switch an aid OFF, which is what makes an aid authorable-away, while this can only ever mark MORE, on a camera nobody is looking through. A capture also **holds the TAILS dark on every ship within `hideTailsWithin` (30) of the LENS** — the long identity streak tuned to be read from across a cell, which at the 8-50u most concepts shoot from is several hull lengths of ribbon crossing the frame in front of the subject. Nothing about the tail changes in play and a shot from further back still gets it; *distance is what separates the two readings, so distance is what the hold gates on*. Three scoping rules: **tails only, never jets** (a jet reads THRUST, which is a close-range read — hiding it removes the thing that works and keeps the thing that does not); the prism **TRAIL is unreachable from here and must never be** (conserved mass is never hidden, and it is not a `TrailRenderer` at all); and it is **scoped to the MARKERS** through `VesselTailAndJets.HideTails`, since a sweep of a vessel's `TrailRenderer`s takes the Rhino's five blade tracers with it. It hides by `Renderer.forceRenderingOff`, never by disabling the component — **a disabled `TrailRenderer` stops RECORDING as well as drawing**, so the ribbon bridges the gap with a straight segment on release, which is a visible artefact left behind by a hold that is supposed to leave nothing. A capture also **prefers a clear line of sight** — it rolls the VANTAGE (never the concept, which would collapse the library onto whichever shot type looks at open space) `clearShotSamples` times and keeps the one with the least prism mass between the lens and the subject, stopping early when one is clear; a preference rather than a rule, so a shot taken deep inside a forest still comes out framed from wherever the mass was thinnest. The occluder test is `PrismSpatialIndex.CountInCone` — **the occlusion corridor's own volume**, a cone rather than a capsule because a prism a metre off the axis at the lens fills the frame while the same prism at the far end barely clips the silhouette — and it **stops one hull radius short of the subject**, which is what lets a ship sit in and among the blocks as freely as it likes: what ruins a shot is a prism BETWEEN the lens and the hull. Captures are named **`<Concept>_<YYYY-MM-DD>_<HH-mm>.png`** — the shot leads (that is what you triage a folder by), the date-time trails and still sorts, and SECONDS are deliberately gone because a filename is read by a person: the precision that made two captures unique is the precision that made every name unreadable. That moves the uniqueness out of the format and into the folder (`ResolveUniquePath` — the first capture of a minute keeps the clean name, a later one takes `_2`, `_3`), since *a screenshot you took and no longer have is worse than one with an ugly name*; `fileNamePrefix` was retired with the seconds, because a constant `CosmicShore_` spends the most valuable characters in the name on the one fact the path already carries. They land in **`<repo>/Recordings`**, resolved per machine off `Application.dataPath`'s parent rather than hardcoded, so one default is right in every clone — and **they are never pushed**, because `/Recordings` is already in `.gitignore` (a screenshot is for the person who took it to triage, not repository content; a test pins the default folder name to that ignore rule so the pair cannot drift apart).
